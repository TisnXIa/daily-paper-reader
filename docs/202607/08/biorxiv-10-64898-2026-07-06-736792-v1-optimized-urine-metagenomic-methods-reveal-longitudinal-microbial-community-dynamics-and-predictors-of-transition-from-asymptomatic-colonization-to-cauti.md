## 摘要
背景：尿路感染（UTIs）是全球最常见的感染之一，其中许多与留置导尿管有关。我们先前对长期导尿的养老院居民进行的基于培养的纵向评估揭示了病原体的持续无症状定植，并表明CAUTI的发生不一定是由于获得新的病原体。在本研究中，我们优化了宏基因组学方法，以检查持续定植和向感染转变背后的生态结构。结果：我们对导管尿液样本进行了全面的纵向宏基因组分析，揭示了来自9名个体的198个样本中69种微生物物种的定植动态。将描述性生态指标与贝叶斯混合效应模型（考虑了参与者内重复采样）相结合，以识别共现物种的聚类，确定抗生素暴露和导管更换等扰动对群落结构的影响，并识别预测感染体征和症状发作的分类群。纵向样本分为三种主要生态表型：1）中等多样性、不稳定的群落（3名参与者）；2）高多样性、稳定的群落，即使在多次导管更换后也能抵抗干扰（3名参与者）；3）低多样性、病原体主导的群落（3名参与者）。仅更换导管并未显著破坏群落组成，而抗生素暴露则引起重大变化，通常在随后几周内被同一属重新定植。鉴定出六个物种簇，其相对丰度在微生物群落的扰动中相互关联，包括一个互斥的肠杆菌目簇和一组难养-厌氧菌簇。发现24种物种与感染体征和症状的出现相关，其中11种被标准尿液培养漏检。结论：导管定植的尿路代表了一个新颖的生态系统，它对导管更换具有抵抗力，但对抗生素扰动敏感。抗生素暴露确实耗尽了所有与体征和症状相关的物种，但也耗尽了潜在的良性微生物。我们的发现对长期导尿患者的导管管理方案和抗生素管理具有直接意义。在更大队列中使用该框架进行前瞻性评估有助于将这些生态学见解转化为临床决策工具。

## Abstract
Background: Urinary tract infections (UTIs) rank among the most common infections globally, with many linked to indwelling urinary catheters. Our prior culture-based longitudinal evaluation of long-term catheterized nursing home residents revealed persistent asymptomatic colonization by pathogens and demonstrated that CAUTI onset was not necessarily due to new pathogen acquisition. In this study, we optimized metagenomics methods to examine the ecological structure underlying persistent colonization and the transition to infection. Results: We present a comprehensive longitudinal metagenomic analysis of catheterized urine specimens, revealing colonization dynamics of 69 microbial species across 198 samples from 9 individuals. Descriptive ecological metrics were combined with Bayesian mixed-effects models that accounted for repeated within-participant sampling to identify clusters of co-occurring species, determine the impact of perturbations such as antibiotic exposure and catheter changes on community structure, and identify taxa predictive of infection sign and symptom onset. Longitudinal specimens clustered into three main ecological phenotypes: 1) moderate diversity, unstable communities (3 participants); 2) high diversity, stable communities that resisted disruption even after multiple catheter changes (3 participants); and 3) low diversity, pathogen-dominated communities (3 participants). Catheter changes alone did not significantly disrupt community composition, while antibiotic exposures induced major shifts often followed by re-colonization with the same genera within subsequent weeks. Six clusters of species were identified for which relative abundances correlated across perturbations to the microbial community, including a mutually exclusive Enterobacterales cluster and fastidious-anaerobe group cluster. 24 species were found to correlate with onset of signs and symptoms of infection, 11 of which were missed by standard urine culture. Conclusions. The catheterized urinary tract represents a novel ecosystem that is resilient to disruption by catheter changes but susceptible to antibiotic perturbation. Antibiotic exposure did deplete all species associated with signs and symptoms but also depleted potentially benign microbes. Our findings have direct implications for catheter management protocols and antibiotic stewardship in long-term catheterized patients. Prospective evaluation using this framework in a larger cohort can help translate these ecological insights into clinical decision-making tools.
