Abstract 18

rapid, complete, and cost-effective genome assemblies in a single workflow. However, the adoption 21 

of standalone ONT sequencing of native DNA has been slowed by concerns that DNA modifications 22 

can compromise per-base sequencing accuracy and downstream genotyping. 23

Impact statement 55 

This study demonstrates that standalone Oxford Nanopore sequencing of native DNA can achieve 56 

highly accurate genotyping for routine foodborne pathogen surveillance across diverse species. We 57 

show that the remaining inaccuracies are linked to specific DNA modification systems, including 58 

phosphorothioation and 7-deazaguanine modifications, which are identified here as previously 59 

unrecognised sources of systematic sequencing errors. To address this limitation, we introduce 60 

alpaqa, a reference-free method for detecting such error-prone assemblies, providing a practical 61 

quality-control framework for ONT-only workflows. Together, these results support the reliable use of 62 

nanopore sequencing in routine genomic surveillance. 63

## Introduction 64 

Whole-genome sequencing (WGS) has revolutionized our ability to understand, track, and combat 65 

bacterial pathogens. By enabling the discrimination between closely related strains, reconstruction of 66 

transmission routes, and detection of antimicrobial resistance and virulence determinants, WGS has 67 

become central to modern pathogen surveillance and cross-border outbreak detection [1, 2]. The 68 

integration of WGS into routine surveillance is a strategic priority for public health agencies worldwide 69 

to strengthen genomic surveillance and outbreak response [3–5]. For over a decade, Illumina short-70 

read sequencing has been the gold standard for WGS for pathogen surveillance due to its high per-71 

base accuracy and has enabled harmonised genomic typing approaches across public health 72 

laboratories, facilitating international data sharing and coordinated outbreak investigations. However, 73 

short reads frequently fail to resolve repetitive regions and structural variants, resulting in fragmented 74 

assemblies [6]. 75 

76 

Long-read nanopore sequencing by Oxford Nanopore Technologies (ONT) overcomes this limitation 77 

by enabling the complete assembly of bacterial chromosomes and plasmids. In addition to improved 78 

genome reconstruction, ONT offers an accessible and cost-effective infrastructure, rapid turnaround 79 

times, and scalable throughput, making it an attractive platform for pathogen surveillance [7]. Early 80 

ONT versions suffered from high error rates that required polishing with short-read data to achieve 81 

reliable assembly accuracy [8]. However, recent advances in flow cell technology (R10.4), chemistry, 82 

basecalling, and polishing algorithms have substantially enhanced raw read and consensus accuracy. 83 

High-quality bacterial genomes can now be generated from ONT data alone, with accuracy levels for 84 

core genome multi-locus sequence typing (cgMLST) and variant calling approaching – and in repetitive 85 

regions sometimes exceeding – Illumina-based approaches [9–14]. As a result, nanopore sequencing 86 

is transitioning from a complementary technology to a standalone platform for genomic surveillance, 87 

offering advantages for both clinical and food safety surveillance where rapid and reproducible 88 

genomic typing is essential for outbreak detection and source attribution [15, 16]. 89

90 

Despite technological improvements, concerns remain regarding the potential impact of epigenetic 91 

DNA modifications, such as methylations, on ONT basecalling accuracy. Although improved basecalling 92 

and polishing algorithms account for common modifications, rare or lineage-specific modification 93 

systems may still introduce systematic assembly errors that could lead to the incorrect exclusion of 94 

epidemiologically related isolates from outbreak clusters [17–19]. The prevalence and impact of such 95 

systems among diverse foodborne pathogens remain insufficiently characterised. Comprehensive 96 

evaluations across large and genetically diverse isolate collections are needed to determine the 97 

reliability and limitations of ONT-only WGS for routine genomic surveillance workflows. 98 

99 

In this study, we systematically evaluate the accuracy of standalone ONT-based WGS across 294 100 

isolates representing diverse lineages of ten major foodborne bacterial pathogens commonly targeted 101 

in public health surveillance. Using read downsampling, we assess the impact of sequencing depth on 102 

assembly accuracy and compare the current high-accuracy (HAC) and super-accuracy (SUP) dorado 103 

basecalling models, which differ in their trade-off between computational efficiency and basecalling 104 

accuracy. We identify atypical DNA modification systems associated with systematic sequencing 105 

errors, highlighting gaps in current basecaller training datasets. To address this challenge, we 106 

introduce alpaqa, a computational approach that detects unreliable assemblies directly from 107 

consensus quality scores without requiring reference genomes or short-read validation, supporting 108 

the use of ONT sequencing in routine foodborne pathogen surveillance. 109

Assembly accuracy remains high at reduced sequencing depth and with HAC@v5.2 basecalling 161 

To estimate the impact of lower sequencing depths on assembly accuracies, we downsampled ONT 162 

reads to 40x and 30x coverage. Most assemblies (280/294, 95.2%) remained highly accurate (≤3 163 

mismatching alleles) even at 30x coverage ( Figure 1 ). Basecalling with the high-accuracy HAC@v5.2 164 

model also produced largely reliable assemblies, with 92.2 % (271/294) maintaining ≤3 mismatching 165 

alleles at 50x depth, although overall accuracy was lower than with the SUP model. 166 

167 

High error rates in dnd + and dpd + assemblies from HAC@v6.0 basecalled data 168 

Basecalling with the HAC@v6.0 model resulted in 93.5 % (275/294) assemblies with ≤3 mismatching 169 

alleles at 50x depth. Whereas assemblies from isolates with methylation systems or no detected 170 

modification systems had accuracies comparable to those generated with SUP@v5.2, all 13 assemblies 171 

with dnd or dpd modification systems consistently exhibited high error rates (24 to 314 mismatching 172 

alleles, median 78) ( Supplementary Table S1 ). To assess the broader relevance of these findings, we 173 

screened publicly available genome assemblies from 67 clinically important bacterial pathogens. We 174 

detected dnd systems in 44 of 67 bacterial species, with prevalence rates of more than 10% in several 175 

species such as Mycobacterium abscessus , Citrobacter koseri , and Clostridioides difficile (Supplement 176 

S3 ). dpd systems were largely restricted to Enterobacterales, with prevalence rates of up to 8% in 177 

several Klebsiella , Escherichia , and Enterobacter species. 178

Detection of unreliable assemblies using the alpaqa quality assessment tool 180 

Our analysis confirms that ONT sequencing with SUP basecalling achieves a high accuracy for >95% of 181 

the investigated isolates at commonly used sequencing depths. However, the remaining inaccurate 182 

assemblies represent a challenge for routine surveillance. Currently, detecting such unreliable 183 

assemblies requires Illumina data for cross-validation, limiting ONT’s use as a standalone method. To 184 

address this, we developed alpaqa, a lightweight bioinformatic tool designed to flag error-prone 185

Masking of low-quality bases improves cgMLST accuracy of error-prone assemblies 231 

To evaluate whether masking low-quality bases improves genotyping accuracy, assemblies were 232 

processed using fastq2a prior to cgMLST analyses. Because masked bases ("N") are excluded from 233 

allele calling in pyMLST or Ridom SeqSphere, this approach converts potential sequencing errors into 234 

missing data. 235 

236 

For the four inaccurate L. monocytogenes assemblies, masking bases with Q≤10 excluded 18 to 27 237 

error-associated loci (~1.5 % of 1701 cgMLST targets) and reduced mismatches to ≤1 allele. For the 238 

four inaccurate S. enterica assemblies, masking bases with Q≤10 excluded 82 to 205 error-associated 239 

loci (~3 to 7 % of 3002 targets) and reduced mismatches to 4 to 12 alleles. Here, achieving near-240 

accurate profiles (0 to 4 mismatches) required a stricter threshold (Q≤15), but this excluded 241 

substantially more loci (178 [5.9%] to 372 [12.4%]), reducing cgMLST resolution ( Supplement S5 ). 242 

243 

Our fully automated ONT assembly pipeline, BOAP, integrates alpaqa-based quality assessments and 244 

the masking of low-quality bases in unreliable assemblies to enable standardised ONT-based genomic 245 

surveillance. 246 

## Discussion 247 

Current ONT sequencing workflows enable highly accurate WGS for pathogen surveillance. Across 294 248 

genetically diverse bacterial isolates representing ten major foodborne pathogens, 97.3% of the ONT-249 

only assemblies from SUP@v5.2 data produced identical or near-identical cgMLST profiles compared 250 

to Illumina-polished references. While these results were achieved at a moderate sequencing depth 251 

of 50x coverage, most assemblies (95.2%) remained highly accurate even at 30x coverage. Increasing 252

sequencing depth beyond 50x provided only marginal improvements. These findings demonstrate that 253 

ONT sequencing is sufficiently robust for routine cgMLST-based surveillance without requiring 254 

excessive sequencing depth. 255

Despite the overall high accuracy, systematic errors persisted in a small subset of SUP@v5.2 257 

assemblies and were associated with atypical epigenetic modifications. At 50x coverage, affected 258 

assemblies showed up to 68 discrepant cgMLST alleles, compromising outbreak and transmission 259 

inference. Importantly, such error-prone assemblies are not readily identifiable using standard 260 

assembly metrics and may therefore remain undetected in routine ONT-only workflows. To address 261 

this limitation, we developed alpaqa, a computational tool that identifies unreliable assemblies 262 

directly from ONT data based on consensus quality score distributions. This approach enables 263 

reference-free detection of problematic assemblies without requiring orthogonal Illumina validation, 264 

removing a major barrier to the adoption of ONT sequencing as a standalone platform for genomic 265 

surveillance, including for previously understudied pathogens. 266 

267 

Several strategies can be applied once unreliable assemblies are identified. Masking low-quality bases 268 

in assemblies results in their exclusion during cgMLST analysis, substantially improving cgMLST 269 

accuracy. However, this approach reduces the number of callable loci and thereby decreases 270 

genotyping resolution, which may limit discriminatory power for outbreak investigations, particularly 271 

in highly error-prone assemblies. Alternatively, affected isolates may be re-sequenced using Illumina 272 

or PCR-based ONT library preparation, which removes native DNA modifications and restores high 273 

assembly accuracy [24]. Given the low frequency of problematic isolates (<3%) observed across a 274 

diverse pathogen collection, routine amplification-based sequencing is generally not desirable, as it 275 

reduces read length, assembly contiguity, and overall workflow efficiency [24]. Instead, selective 276 

application of targeted mitigation strategies guided by quality assessment tools may represent a more 277 

practical solution. We implemented the fully automated Nextflow pipeline BOAP integrating assembly, 278

Excessive error rates in our assemblies were associated with atypical DNA modifications. Whereas 283 

elevated error rates with the SUP@v5.2 model were limited to four S. enterica serovar Kentucky 284 

isolates carrying a dnd phosphorothioation system targeting GGCC motifs, the HAC@v6.0 model 285 

generated highly inaccurate assemblies for all 13 isolates carrying either dnd or dpd systems, 286 

irrespective of the targeted sequence motif or modification type. This finding suggests that these DNA 287 

modifications were absent or underrepresented in the dataset used to train the HAC@v6.0 basecalling 288 

model. Dnd-mediated phosphorothioation and Dpd-mediated 7-deazaguanine modifications are 289 

sufficiently widespread in important bacterial pathogens that associated sequencing errors are likely 290 

to be encountered in routine genomic surveillance. 291 

292 

In addition, elevated error rates were observed in four L. monocytogenes isolates carrying 293 

methyltransferases targeting GAAGAC and GCNGC motifs. These methylation-associated errors were 294 

previously reported [18] but were substantially reduced by improved polishing with medaka v2. 295 

Nevertheless, residual motif-specific errors persisted, highlighting the continued influence of DNA 296 

modifications on nanopore sequencing accuracy. Beyond methylation, phosphorothioation, and 7-297 

deazaguanine modifications, additional DNA modification systems likely exist and remain to be 298 

discovered. [23, 25]. Tools such as alpaqa may therefore not only support assembly quality control 299 

but also facilitate the identification of previously unrecognized modification-associated error 300 

signatures. 301

ONT sequencing offers several practical advantages over traditional short-read platforms, including 303 

rapid turnaround times, flexible throughput, and reduced infrastructure requirements [7]. These 304

This study has several limitations. Although the dataset includes a large number of isolates across 314 

major foodborne pathogens, the sample size was limited for some species. Rare DNA modification 315 

systems present at low prevalence within certain species may not have been captured. In addition, 316 

Illumina and ONT sequencing were performed on independent DNA extracts. While this does not 317 

influence the systematic error patterns investigated here, it could in rare cases contribute to isolated 318 

allelic differences. Finally, our analyses were based on current nanopore chemistries and basecalling 319 

models. Future basecalling models that include more diverse DNA modification contexts may further 320 

reduce motif-associated errors identified in this study. 321 

322 

In conclusion, our results show that standalone ONT sequencing can provide highly accurate whole-323 

genome assemblies suitable for routine pathogen surveillance. While rare epigenetic modifications 324 

may still introduce systematic assembly errors, these can be detected using the alpaqa computational 325 

framework, enabling correction strategies such as masking error-prone positions. Together, these 326 

findings support the transition of nanopore sequencing from a complementary technology to a 327 

standalone platform for high-resolution genomic surveillance of bacterial pathogens. 328

## Methods 329 

Isolate selection and whole genome sequencing 330 

A total of 294 isolates representing ten major foodborne pathogens were selected for this study. The 331 

collection comprised Bacillus cereus group members, Campylobacter coli , Campylobacter jejuni ,332 

Cronobacter sakazakii , Listeria monocytogenes , Salmonella enterica , Shigella flexneri , Shigella sonnei ,333 

Vibrio parahaemolyticus , and Yersinia enterocolitica (Supplementary Table S1 ). Most isolates were 334 

collected in Switzerland and had previously been Illumina-sequenced for research or surveillance 335 

purposes at the Institute for Food Safety and Hygiene, Zurich, Switzerland. Isolates were selected 336 

based on existing Illumina data to represent a broad diversity of serovars and multi-locus sequence 337 

types (MLSTs) relevant to food safety. Within the B. cereus group, both cereulide-producing strains 338

Standalone assembly quality assessment 396 

To identify assemblies potentially impacted by systematic basecalling errors, we developed alpaqa 397 

(“Assembly-Level Profiling And Quality Assessment”). The tool processes fastq assembly files 398 

generated with dorado polish or medaka v2 and identifies low-quality bases (LQB), defined here as 399 

bases with a Phred quality score between 1 and 5. A score of 0 was excluded to prevent bias from 400 

regions lacking quality information, such as assembly artefacts. The algorithm first masks LQB-dense 401 

regions: to avoid overcounting errors in repetitive or poorly resolved genomic regions, a sliding 402 

window (5,000 bp) scans the longest contig. Regions are masked if their LQB density exceeds five times 403 

the baseline density of the contig (minimum 0.1% density). After masking, alpaqa calculates a 404 

normalized LQB count per Megabase (Mbp) for the remaining high-quality regions. In addition, alpaqa 405
