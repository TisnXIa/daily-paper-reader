## 摘要
蛭弧菌及其类似生物（BALOs）是专性细菌捕食者，通过促进物种多样性来塑造微生物群落，但长期以来，由于它们被认为具有专性好氧的生活方式，被认为与人类肠道无关。在此，我们通过结合荟萃分析、实验和概念研究，挑战了这一观点。重新分析了来自人类微生物组纲要的168,000个一致处理的样本（涵盖482项研究），我们在80多项研究中检测到BALOs，分布于全球多个身体部位，肠道流行率为2.4%，这一结果通过重新分析PRIME数据库的16S rRNA微生物组数据得到证实。引人注目的是，BALOs的存在与身体部位和疾病背景下的更高微生物α多样性始终相关。活检样本显示其流行率明显高于粪便样本，表明其存在于黏膜近端的小生境中。我们的实验室实验表明，多种蛭弧菌菌株可以在体外延缓微生物多样性的丧失，并在肠道相关条件下（包括37°C、pH 6.5和存在黏液）保持活性。基因组分析进一步揭示了多个BALO基因组中含有末端还原酶，包括亚硝酸盐还原酶，表明具有厌氧或微需氧呼吸的能力，与在黏膜微环境中的持续存在一致。值得注意的是，培养的BALOs的代谢和生态特征与兼性厌氧菌高度匹配，后者是其首选猎物，也是炎症性肠病、糖尿病、结直肠癌和慢性肾病中生态失调的核心驱动因素。基于这些发现，我们提出了一个概念框架，其中BALOs通过控制炎症条件下兼性厌氧菌的扩张来促进肠道稳态，从而有助于恢复发酵产丁酸的群落。综合来看，我们的结果确立了BALOs作为人类微生物组中一致且功能相关的成员，并作为针对慢性肠道疾病的治疗策略中一个有前途的天然候选者。

## Abstract
Bdellovibrio and like organisms (BALOs) are obligate bacterial predators that shape microbial communities by promoting species diversity, yet they have long been considered irrelevant to the human gut due to their presumed obligate aerobic lifestyle. Here, we challenge this view through a combined meta-analytic, experimental, and conceptual investigation of BALOs in human microbiomes. Reanalyzing 168,000 consistently processed samples from the Human Microbiome Compendium spanning 482 studies, we detected BALOs in more than 80 studies and across multiple body sites worldwide, with a gut prevalence of 2.4%, a finding confirmed by reanalysis of the PRIME database for 16S rRNA microbiome data. Strikingly, BALO presence was consistently associated with higher microbial alpha-diversity across body sites and disease contexts. Biopsy-derived samples showed a substantially higher prevalence than fecal samples, suggesting a mucosa-proximal niche. Our laboratory experiments showed that multiple Bdellovibrio strains can delay the loss of microbial diversity in vitro and remain active under gut-relevant conditions, including 37C, pH 6.5, and in the presence of mucus. Genomic analyses further revealed terminal reductases, including nitrite reductases, in several BALO genomes, indicating the capacity for anaerobic or microaerobic respiration, consistent with persistence in mucosal microenvironments. Notably, the metabolic and ecological profiles of cultured BALOs closely match those of facultative anaerobes, which constitute their preferred prey and are central drivers of dysbiosis in inflammatory bowel disease, diabetes, colorectal cancer, and chronic kidney disease. Building on these findings, we propose a conceptual framework in which BALOs contribute to gut homeostasis by controlling the expansion of facultative anaerobes under inflammatory conditions, thereby facilitating the restoration of fermentative, butyrate-producing communities. Together, our results establish BALOs as consistent, functionally relevant members of the human microbiome and a promising natural candidate for therapeutic strategies targeting chronic gut disease.
