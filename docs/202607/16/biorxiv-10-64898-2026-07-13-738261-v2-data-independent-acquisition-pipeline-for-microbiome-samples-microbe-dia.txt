Abstract 10 

The functional complexity inherent in microbiomes complicates analytical approaches 11 

aimed at defining phenotype. As proteins are the functional effectors of microbiome 12 

phenotype s, improving the performance of mass spectrometry -based meta proteomics is 13 

critical to achieving the functional characterization of these systems. Data -independent 14 

acquisition (DIA) improve s protein coverage and reduce s data missing ness when 15 

compared to data -dependent acquisition (DDA ) in metaproteomic s. However, the 16 

application of DIA to complex microbial systems remains constrained by analytical 17 

throughput and computational scalability. Here, we optimize d LC –MS/MS acquisition 18 

parameters for both DDA and DIA using a model microbiome , demonstrating how DIA 19 

enables increased sample throughput without compromising quantitative performance . In 20

Introduction 30 

Environmental microbiome samples posses s a highly complex proteomic profile . This 31 

complexity arise s from two sources: variation in the abundance of the microbial species as 32 

well as variation in the abundance of the proteins in each of the microbes. Further, each 33 

microbial genome typically contains thousands of protein -coding genes which are highly 34 

ho molo gous across microbes, creating redundant ly expressed proteins and peptides .35 

Metaproteomics aims to detect and quantify the suite of proteins expressed in microbial 36 

co mmunities , contributing to our understanding of critical functional modulations in these 37 

systems and enabling comprehensive multi -omics integratio n [1] .38 

Liquid chromatography tandem mass spectrometry (LC -MS/MS) proteomic analysis 39 

focuses on the evaluation of tryptic peptides from samples where fragmentation of intact 40

Sample preparation for proteomics analysis. The washed cell pellets were lysed on a 107 

bead mill homogenizer (Bead Ruptor Elite) under settings: Speed 6.3 for 45 seconds. 108 

Lysates were then centrifuged at 3220 x rpm in large centrifuge for 10min at 4C to remove 109 

debris. The supernatant were then transferred to fresh tubes with urea to achieve a final 110 

concentration of 8M. A BCA assay (Thermo Fisher Scientific) was then used to estimate 111 

protein concentration for downstream processing. Proteins were then reduced by 112 

incubating at 10 mM dithiothreitol (DTT) at 37 ºC for 1 hr. Lysates were then diluted 8 -fold 113 

to reduce the urea concentration prior to digestion with trypsin at a 1:50 enzyme:substrate 114 

ratio, incubating at 37 ºC for 3 hrs. The digestion reaction was then quenched by plunge 115 

freezing in LN 2. Peptides were then desalted using strata C18 cartridges (Phenomenex). 116 

Eluents were dried in a speed vac and rehydrated in 50 μL of 18 MΩ water. The 117 

concentration of peptides was measured by BCA assay and diluted to 0.1 μg/μL for 118 

downstream sample creation (Supplementary Table 1 and 2). The resulting peptide 119 

samples were then mixed as described in Supplementary Tables 1, 2, and 3 to create the 120 

model microbiomes for our studies. 121

Simulating microbiome complexity . The goal of this study was to develop and validate a 267 

robust workflow using DIA -based proteomics for the characterization of microbiomes. We 268 

used mixtures of peptides isolated from axenic cultured microbial species to model 269 

microbiome -like complexity, where microbial species composition and concentrations 270 

were predetermined. To design these mixtures and to ensure they reflected realistic 271 

complexity, we assessed the phylogenetic relatedness of the available species. In total, we 272 

used 46 strain isolates from 28 bacterial species originating from a soil microbiome [15] .273 

The full list of strains and their genome characteristics is provided in Supplementary 274 

Tables 1 & 2. We utilized previously cultured and sequenced organisms and performed in 275 

silico trypsin digestion on their individual FASTA files. In -silico peptide sequences from all 276 

strains of each species were merged into single -species level lists. The relatedness of the 277 

species was measured as the percent of tryptic peptide sequences shared with any of the 278 

other available species (Supplementary Figure 1). This analysis indicated that there is a 279

The main goals of metaproteomics are (i) to identify which organisms are present and 475

large database searches [58] . MADL builds on this concept by improving the stepwise logic 483

deposited on Github at TBD , allowing people to reproduce our approach. 485

Discussion 545 

Prior work has shown that data -independent acquisition (DIA) offers clear advantages over 546 

data -dependent acquisition (DDA), including improved quantification and reduced 547 

missingness, even in the complex case of metaproteomics. However, most studies stop 548 

short of defining a complete, metaproteomics -specific DIA workflow that spans from LC -549 

MS/MS data acquisition through to data analysis. A frequently cited obstacle to a fully 550 

DDA -independent pipeline is the computational burden of library -free approaches when 551 

applied to very large metaproteomic databases, even though these methods have proven 552 

effective in more limited, well -defined systems. 553 

Within this context, we systematically evaluated DIA acquisition parameters and found 554 

that MS2 window size is a major determinant of DIA identification performance, whereas 555 

refinement of the MS1 m/z range has a relatively minor impact on the number of proteins 556 

and peptides identified. A smaller MS2 window size appears to be preferable for complex 557 

metaproteomic samples, likely because it reduces peptide interferences in the already 558 

complex DIA spectra. As DIA scoring algorithms continue to improve, larger MS2 window 559 

sizes may enable higher throughput without sacrificing proteome depth. As LC -MS/MS 560 

instrumentation evolves, we anticipate incremental increase in throughput in 561 

metaproteome analyses, as demonstrated by the substantially higher identification yield 562 

on the newer Astral instruments compared to the older Exploris. The comparable number 563 

of correctly quantified proteins on the faster Astral instrument suggests that aggressive 564

these refinements in LC -MS/MS DIA can enable study designs with many more biological 566

throughput creates a powerful synergy for the age of AI; by generating massive datasets 568 

required for machine learning, our workflow enables the discovery of subtle, complex 569 

patterns in microbiome function that were previously beyond our analytical reach. 570 

DIA’s ability to recover information is comparable to deeply fractionated DDA data, but in 571 

an order -of -magnitude less instrument time, makes it the practical choice for experiments 572 

focused on broad functional profiling and comparative analyses across many samples. 573 

However, fractionated DDA data may remain the preferred method when fewer samples 574 

are being processed or when cleaner MS/MS for maximal sequence coverage is required, 575 

for instance, in novel protein or post -translational modification (PTM) discovery. Future 576 

work should focus on characterizing the physicochemical properties of the proteins or 577 

peptides uniquely identified by DDA to determine if they represent a specific biological 578 

class that is systematically under sampled by DIA. Aside from such targeted objectives, 579 

the marginal gain in identifications from extensively fractionated DDA is small relative to 580 

the cost in instrument time and sample consumption. Thus, for the majority of modern 581 

metaproteomic studies, focused on systems -level comparison, DIA represents the most 582 

judicious investment of analytical resources. 583

Conclusions 623 

In conclusion, we establish the Metaproteomic Analysis with DIA Library -free (MADL) 624 

pipeline as a practical and scalable approach for high -throughput metaproteomic 625
