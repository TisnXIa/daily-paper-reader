## 摘要
宏基因组测序的进展改变了我们对宿主相关微生物组的理解，揭示了其在免疫调节、营养代谢和胃肠道稳态中的关键作用。然而，大型非模式物种，尤其是大象的肠道微生物组仍鲜有研究。圈养引入了饮食、环境和管理相关的干扰，可能破坏微生物平衡并导致胃肠功能障碍。

在此，我们对俄勒冈动物园五只圈养亚洲象（Elephas maximus）的粪便微生物组进行了纵向分析，这些大象表现出慢性粪便异常，包括黏液和粪便成形不稳定。在14周内，每周收集粪便样本，并与来自其他三家动物园的临床正常大象样本进行比较。使用16S rRNA基因测序，我们发现受影响大象和对照大象之间的微生物群落组成存在显著差异。俄勒冈动物园大象的菌群失调特征表现为Akkermansia muciniphila和多个梭菌目成员的富集，包括先前与胃肠疾病相关的分类群。

使用市售益生菌配方与粪便特征的短暂改善和微生物组成的显著变化相关，包括治疗后总微生物多样性显著降低以及与胃肠道异常相关的多个分类群丰度下降。值得注意的是，益生菌菌株本身未被检测到，表明其具有间接或短暂的功能效应，而非持久定植。

总之，这些发现首次对圈养亚洲象的肠道微生物组失调进行了纵向表征，并识别了与圈养条件下慢性胃肠功能障碍相关的候选微生物贡献者，这对大型动物的饲养管理、饮食管理和基于微生物组的干预具有启示意义。此外，我们的研究强调了益生菌在治疗单胃大型脊椎动物胃肠疾病时的潜在但有限且可能是间接的益处。

## Abstract
Advances in metagenomic sequencing have transformed our understanding of host-associated microbiota, revealing critical roles in immune regulation, nutrient metabolism, and gastrointestinal (GI) homeostasis. However, the gut microbiome of large non-model species, particularly elephants, remains poorly characterized. Captivity introduces dietary, environmental, and management-related perturbations that may disrupt microbial balance and contribute to GI dysfunction.

Here, we performed a longitudinal analysis of the fecal microbiome in five captive Asian elephants (Elephas maximus) at the Oregon Zoo exhibiting chronic fecal abnormalities, including mucus and inconsistent stool formation. Over 14 weeks, weekly fecal samples were collected and compared with samples from clinically normal elephants housed at three other zoological institutions. Using 16S rRNA gene sequencing, we identified marked differences in microbial community composition between affected and control elephants. Dysbiosis in Oregon Zoo elephants was characterized by enrichment of Akkermansia muciniphila and multiple members of the order Clostridiales, including taxa previously associated with gastrointestinal disorders.

Administration of a commercially available probiotic formulation was associated with transient improvement in fecal characteristics and pronounced shifts in microbial composition, including a significant post-treatment reduction in overall microbial diversity and decreased abundance of several taxa linked to GI abnormalities. Notably, probiotic strains themselves were not detected, suggesting indirect or short-lived functional effects rather than durable colonization.

Together, these findings provide one of the first longitudinal characterizations of gut microbiome dysbiosis in captive Asian elephants and identify candidate microbial contributors to chronic GI dysfunction in captivity, with implications for husbandry, dietary management, and microbiome-informed interventions in megafauna. Additionally, our study underscores the potential, although limited and likely indirect, benefit of probiotics when treating GI disorders in monograstric megavertebrates.
