## 摘要
动物肠道中的微生物竞争定殖于空间受限的宿主生态位。然而，空间竞争是否驱动宿主间的个体间变异，以及哪些因素决定了这种竞争的结果，仍然知之甚少。本研究调查了西方蜜蜂肠道共生菌Frischella perrara与蜜蜂肠道微生物群其他成员的竞争相互作用。对单个蜜蜂的鸟枪法宏基因组学分析显示，F. perrara与Gilliamella属的一个特定物种呈负相关。将这两种细菌共同定殖于微生物群耗尽的蜜蜂中，导致它们竞争性排斥。这种竞争的结果取决于每只蜜蜂接收到的细菌相对数量，并且当F. perrara的两个VI型分泌系统之一发生突变时，竞争结果有利于Gilliamella。利用荧光标记菌株、高分辨率显微镜和肠道区域特异性定量，我们显示两种细菌在单一定殖蜜蜂的回肠中定位于相同的宿主生态位，表明在空间受限的宿主生态位中存在竞争。此外，这两种微生物都能抵御感染，促进蜜蜂健康。这种竞争为解释F. perrara在蜜蜂蜂群中发生的变异性提供了机制，并强调了肠道空间结构和微生物竞争在塑造微生物组组成和个体间变异性中的重要性。

## Abstract
Microbes in the animal gut compete to colonize spatially restricted host niches. However, whether competition for space drives inter-individual variation among hosts, and which factors determine the outcome of such competition, remain poorly understood. Here, we investigated competitive interactions of the western honeybee gut symbiont Frischella perrara with other members of the bee gut microbiota. Shotgun metagenomics analysis of individual bees revealed that F. perrara is negatively correlated with a specific species of the genus Gilliamella. Co-colonization of microbiota-depleted bees with these two bacteria resulted in their competitive exclusion. The outcome of this competition depended on the relative number of bacteria each bee received and benefited Gilliamella when one of the two type VI secretion systems of F. perrara was mutated. Using fluorescently tagged strains, high-resolution microscopy, and gut region-specific quantification, we show that both bacteria localize to the same host niche in the ileum of mono-colonized bees, indicating competition in a spatially restricted host niche. Moreover, both microbes protected against infection, promoting bee health. This competition provides an explanatory mechanism underlying variation in the occurrence of F. perrara across honeybee colonies and highlights the importance of gut spatial structure and microbial competition in shaping microbiome composition and inter-individual variability.
