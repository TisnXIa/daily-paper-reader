## 摘要
温度是生物系统的一个基本约束，但它如何反映在基因组序列组织中仍不清楚。在这里，我们展示全基因组短DNA序列分布包含一个热适应的稳健信号，该信号在很大程度上独立于系统发育。使用结构主题建模（STM），一种用于识别共现序列基序组的机器学习方法，我们分析了细菌和古菌基因组代理（随机抽样的基因组区域）的标准6-mer和9-mer频率谱，并识别出与嗜热菌和嗜冷菌系统关联的基序家族。在细菌嗜热菌中，识别的基序家族以高度特异性、过度表达和共现的C-和G-堆积六聚体为主导，以及一个在多个温度比较中重复出现的CG周期性六聚体独特家族。相反，细菌嗜冷菌相关基序则以低复杂性的A-、T-和AT-重复六聚体为主导。嗜热古菌通常表现出一个独特的以CTAG为中心的六聚体家族，表明不同域可能通过不同的序列级解决方案适应相似的环境约束。然而，这种域级对比并非绝对：在两个嗜热细菌-古菌对的针对性分析中，我们发现所有STM识别的嗜热菌相关六聚体家族频率异常相似，表明共享的高温环境在特定情况下可以部分覆盖系统发育分歧。值得注意的是，所识别的基序家族仅构成了可能富含G+C或富含A+T序列的广阔空间中的一个微小且高度选择性的子集。这表明热适应与特定的序列架构相关，而非核苷酸组成的广泛变化。因此，观察到的信号不能仅由整体碱基组成解释，而是源于短序列上下文中核苷酸的结构化组合和位置排列。在k=6和k=9下都恢复了相关的基序家族，表明该信号反映了全基因组序列组织的系统性变化，而非孤立的序列基序。这些模式与生化和生物物理研究中记录的已知序列依赖的DNA物理特性一致，包括碱基堆积相互作用和构象灵活性的差异。总之，我们的结果表明，全基因组序列组织反映了与热适应相关的序列依赖的DNA物理特性，揭示了系统发育历史之外一个先前未被充分认识的基因组信息物理层。

## Abstract
Temperature is a fundamental constraint on biological systems, yet how it is reflected in genome sequence organization remains unclear. Here, we show that genome-wide distributions of short DNA sequences contain a robust signal of thermal adaptation that is largely independent of phylogeny. Using Structural Topic Modelling (STM), a machine-learning approach for identifying groups of co-occurring sequence motifs, we analyze canonical 6-mer and 9-mer frequency profiles of bacterial and archaeal genome proxies (randomly sampled genomic regions) and identify motif families systematically associated with thermophiles and psychrophiles. In bacterial thermophiles, the identified motif families are dominated by highly specific, overrepresented and co-occurring C- and G-stacked hexamers, and a distinct family of CG-periodic hexamers recurring across multiple temperature comparisons. In contrast, bacterial psychrophile-associated motifs are dominated by low-complexity A-, T-, and AT-run hexamers. Thermophilic archaea generally exhibit a distinct CTAG-centred hexamer family, suggesting that different domains may adapt to similar environmental constraints through different sequence-level solutions. However, this domain-level contrast is not absolute: in a targeted analysis of two thermophilic bacterium-archaeon pairs, we find unusually similar frequencies of all the STM-identified thermophile-associated hexamer families, suggesting that shared high-temperature environments can, in specific cases, partially override phylogenetic divergence. Notably, the identified motif families constitute only a small and highly selective subset of the vast space of possible G+C-rich or A+T-rich sequences. This indicates that thermal adaptation is associated with specific sequence architectures rather than broad shifts in nucleotide composition. Accordingly, the observed signal cannot be explained by overall base composition alone, but instead arises from structured combinations and positional arrangements of nucleotides within short sequence contexts. Related motif families are recovered at both k = 6 and k = 9, indicating that the signal reflects systematic shifts in genome-wide sequence organization rather than isolated sequence motifs. These patterns are consistent with known sequence-dependent DNA physical properties documented in biochemical and bio-physical studies, including differences in base-stacking interactions and conformational flexibility. Together, our results suggest that genome-wide sequence organization reflects sequence-dependent DNA physical properties associated with thermal adaptation, revealing a previously underappreciated physical layer of genomic information beyond phylogenetic history.
