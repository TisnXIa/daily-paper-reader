## 摘要
全变态多食性昆虫经历变态并利用多种寄主植物，使其在生命阶段和寄主植物上暴露于高度多变的环境条件。这些昆虫是否拥有稳定的核心微生物群、生命阶段特异性或寄主植物特异性微生物群仍然悬而未决。本研究利用16S和ITS宏条形码技术，表征了斑翅果蝇（Drosophila suzukii）在生命阶段（幼虫、蛹和羽化成虫）和寄主果实（樱桃、黑莓和草莓）中相关的真菌和细菌群落。我们基于群落和网络分析，检验了生命阶段和寄主果实对微生物群组成的相对影响。我们验证了实验室操纵个体的微生物群与野外个体的微生物群相似。首先，我们发现酵母菌（而非细菌）在不同寄主果实间具有强烈结构分化。细菌和丝状真菌在不同果实间共享，构成了以Gluconobacter cerinus、Tatumella sp.和Cladosporium spp.为主导的果实核心微生物群。相反，酵母菌则具有果实特异性：Hanseniaspora和Pichia属主要与樱桃和草莓相关，而Metschnikowia属则与黑莓相关。其次，我们发现与斑翅果蝇相关的真菌（而非细菌）群落在不同寄主果实间具有结构分化。斑翅果蝇拥有由G. cerinus组成的核心细菌微生物群，以及由酵母菌构成的生态位特异性（寄主植物特异性）微生物群：与樱桃和草莓相关的个体中典型存在Hanseniaspora，与黑莓相关的个体中典型存在Metschnikowia。核心和生态位特异性微生物群的组成成分最可能是斑翅果蝇从寄主果实水平获取的。综上所述，我们的结果强调了采用元群落方法研究昆虫、寄主植物和微生物群三方相互作用的重要性。

重要性 微生物群在介导植食性昆虫与其寄主植物相互作用中的作用已在专食性物种中得到充分阐明。然而，在多食性（危害多种寄主植物）物种中，以及对于经历不同生态位发育的全变态物种在生命阶段中的研究尚不全面。我们检验了多食性全变态物种斑翅果蝇中是否存在核心、生态位特异性和阶段特异性微生物群。我们检查了危害三种寄主果实的幼虫、蛹和羽化成虫中的真菌和细菌群落。我们的结果显示：首先，果实上细菌、丝状真菌和酵母菌的组装受不同生态过程驱动。其次，斑翅果蝇拥有核心细菌微生物群、由酵母菌构成的生态位特异性微生物群，而无阶段特异性微生物群。我们的研究强调了综合考虑寄主植物和多食性昆虫微生物群组装以更好地理解昆虫-微生物相互作用的重要性。

## Abstract
Holometabolous polyphagous insects undergo metamorphosis and exploit multiple host plants, exposing them to highly variable ecological conditions across both life stages and host plants. Whether these insects harbour a stable core microbiota, a life stage- or a host plant-specific microbiota remain open questions. Here, we characterised fungal and bacterial communities associated with Drosophila suzukii across life stages (larvae, pupae and emerging flies) and host fruits (cherry, blackberry and strawberry) using 16S and ITS metabarcoding. We tested the relative influence of life stage and host fruit on microbiota composition using community and network-based analyses. We verified that the microbiota of manipulated laboratory individuals were similar to the microbiota of individuals from the field. First, we identified that yeasts, but not bacteria, were strongly structured among host fruits. Bacteria and filamentous fungi were shared across fruits, constituting a fruit core microbiota dominated by Gluconobacter cerinus, Tatumella sp. and Cladosporium spp. In contrast, yeast were rather fruit-specific: Hanseniaspora and Pichia genera mostly associated with cherries and strawberries, contrary to Metschnikowia with blackberries. Second, we found that fungal, but not bacterial, communities associated with D. suzukii were structured among host fruits. D. suzukii harboured a core bacterial microbiota composed of G. cerinus and a niche-specific (host plant-specific) microbiota composed of yeasts: Hanseniaspora typical in individuals related to cherry and strawberry, and Metschnikowia to blackberry. Components of both core and niche-specific microbiota were most likely horizontally acquired by D. suzukii from host fruits. Taken together our results underline the importance of meta-community approaches to investigate tripartite interactions among insects, host plants and microbiota.

IMPORTANCEThe role of microbiota in mediating interactions between phytophagous insects and their host plants has been well illustrated in specialist species. However, it has been less comprehensively studied in polyphagous species, which infest multiple host plants, and across life stages for holometabolous species experiencing separate ecological niches through development. We tested the existence of a core, a niche-specific and a stage-specific microbiota in a polyphagous holometabolous species, D. suzukii. We examined both fungal and bacterial communities in larvae, pupae and emerging flies infesting three host fruits. Our results showed first that the assembly of bacteria, filamentous fungi and yeasts on fruits is driven by different ecological processes. Second, that D. suzukii harbours a core bacterial microbiota, a niche-specific microbiota constituted by yeasts and no stage-specific microbiota. Our study emphasizes the importance of considering jointly the assembly of host plant and polyphagous insect microbial communities to better understand insect-microbe interactions.
