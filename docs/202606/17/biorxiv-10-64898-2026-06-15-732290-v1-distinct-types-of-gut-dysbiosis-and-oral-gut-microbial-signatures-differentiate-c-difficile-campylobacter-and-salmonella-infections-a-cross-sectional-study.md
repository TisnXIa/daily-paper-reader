## 摘要
胃肠道细菌感染与肠道微生物群的大量紊乱相关，然而大多数微生物组研究仅孤立地考察单一病原体，限制了识别不同肠道感染之间共享和病原体特异性的微生物标志物。我们利用16S rRNA基因测序对586份粪便样本进行了比较分析，涵盖了由艰难梭菌、弯曲杆菌属和沙门氏菌属引起的感染，以及病毒性胃肠炎、诊断阴性样本和健康对照。在部分样本中测量了粪便钙卫蛋白浓度以评估肠道炎症。比较分析揭示了细菌性肠道感染中的两种主要菌群失调模式。艰难梭菌感染的特征是肠球菌主导的群落结构，而弯曲杆菌和沙门氏菌感染则与包括链球菌、颗粒链菌和嗜血杆菌在内的口腔相关类群紧密关联的协同富集相关。这些类群是XGBoost机器学习分类器中信息量最大的特征之一，该分类器能够准确区分不同类型的细菌感染，以及病毒性感染和健康相关的微生物群谱（宏F1分数=0.74）。相比之下，肠杆菌科的扩增代表了肠道紊乱的一个共享、非特异性标志，并与粪便钙卫蛋白浓度升高相关。总之，这些发现表明细菌性肠道感染与不同的微生物群构型相关，这些构型将病原体特异性标志物与一般感染相关菌群失调区分开来。弯曲杆菌和沙门氏菌感染中口腔相关类群的富集提示口腔-肠道微生物轴在细菌性胃肠炎中的潜在作用，并为未来的机制研究以及基于微生物群的诊断、预防和治疗策略的发展提供了基础。

作者总结：肠道细菌感染可引起严重腹泻和炎症，但其对肠道微生物群落的影响尚未完全了解。以往多数研究仅关注单一致病菌，难以确定哪些微生物组变化是感染共有的，哪些是特定病原体特有的。本研究比较了感染艰难梭菌、弯曲杆菌或沙门氏菌者的肠道微生物群谱，并与病毒性胃肠炎、诊断阴性腹泻患者及健康对照样本一同考察。我们发现细菌性肠道感染与两种不同的微生物群破坏模式相关。艰难梭菌感染与肠球菌过度生长相关，而弯曲杆菌和沙门氏菌感染则以口腔中常见多个细菌类群水平升高为特征。相比之下，肠杆菌科的扩增在不同感染中均有观察到，似乎反映了一般肠道紊乱而非特定病原体。我们的发现识别出区分不同细菌感染的微生物标志物，并提示源自口腔的细菌可能在部分胃肠炎中发挥重要作用。这些结果为未来理解微生物群落如何影响肠道感染和恢复的研究奠定了基础。

## Abstract
Gastrointestinal bacterial infections are associated with substantial perturbations of the gut microbiota, yet most microbiome studies have examined individual pathogens in isolation, limiting identification of shared and pathogen-specific microbial signatures across enteric infections. We performed a comparative analysis of fecal microbiota profiles from 586 stool samples using 16S rRNA gene sequencing, encompassing infections caused by Clostridioides difficile, Campylobacter spp., and Salmonella spp., alongside viral gastroenteritis, diagnostic-negative samples, and healthy controls. Fecal calprotectin concentrations were measured in a subset of samples to assess intestinal inflammation. Comparative analyses revealed two major dysbiotic configurations among bacterial enteric infections. C. difficile infection was characterized by an Enterococcus-dominated community structure, whereas Campylobacter and Salmonella infections were associated with enrichment of a tightly correlated consortium of oral-associated taxa, including Streptococcus, Granulicatella, and Haemophilus. These taxa were among the most informative features in an XGBoost machine-learning classifier, which accurately discriminated bacterial infection types from one another and from viral infections and healthy-associated microbiota profiles (macro F1 score = 0.74). In contrast, expansion of Enterobacteriaceae represented a shared, non-specific signature of intestinal disturbance and was associated with elevated fecal calprotectin concentrations. Together, these findings demonstrate that bacterial enteric infections are associated with distinct microbiota configurations that distinguish pathogen-specific signatures from general infection-related dysbiosis. The enrichment of oral-associated taxa in Campylobacter and Salmonella infections suggests a potential role for the oral-gut microbial axis in bacterial gastroenteritis and provides a foundation for future mechanistic studies and the development of microbiota-informed diagnostic, preventive, and therapeutic strategies.

Author SummaryBacterial infections of the intestine can cause severe diarrhea and inflammation, but their effects on the community of microbes living in the gut are not fully understood. Most previous studies have focused on a single disease-causing organism, making it difficult to determine which microbiome changes are shared across infections and which are specific to particular pathogens. In this study, we compared gut microbiota profiles from people infected with Clostridioides difficile, Campylobacter, or Salmonella, and examined these alongside samples from individuals with viral gastroenteritis, diagnostic-negative diarrhea, and healthy controls. We found that bacterial enteric infections were associated with two distinct patterns of microbiome disruption. C. difficile infection was linked to an overgrowth of Enterococcus, whereas Campylobacter and Salmonella infections were characterized by increased levels of several bacterial groups that are commonly found in the mouth. In contrast, expansion of Enterobacteriaceae was observed across different infections and appeared to reflect general intestinal disturbance rather than a specific pathogen. Our findings identify microbial signatures that distinguish different bacterial infections and suggest that bacteria originating from the oral cavity may play an important role in some forms of gastroenteritis. These results provide a foundation for future studies aimed at understanding how microbial communities influence intestinal infection and recovery.
