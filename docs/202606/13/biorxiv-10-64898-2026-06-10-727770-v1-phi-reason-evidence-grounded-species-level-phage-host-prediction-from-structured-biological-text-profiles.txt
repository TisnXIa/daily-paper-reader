Abstract 14 

Phage–host interaction (PHI) prediction is a fundamental problem in micro-15 

biology. Existing approaches typically encode phage and host information as 16 

numerical representations derived from sequence similarity, protein content or 17 

reference databases, and use them to score candidate hosts or train predic-18 

tion models. Although effective, this design obscures which biological evidence 19 

supports a prediction. Here, we present PHI-Reason, a species-level PHI predic-20 

tion framework that reformulates host prediction as inference over structured 21 

biological text. PHI-Reason converts heterogeneous genomic, functional and con-22 

textual evidence into modular natural-language profiles, which a frozen large 23 

language model integrates at inference time for candidate-host ranking or pair-24 

wise PHI assessment. Across species-level benchmarks, PHI-Reason achieved 25 

competitive performance and complementary correct assignments relative to 26 

established methods. Its explicit profile design enables evidence perturbation 27 

and rationale-grounding analyses, making prediction support and hallucination 28 

risk operationally measurable. PHI-Reason provides an interpretable evidence-29 

integration approach and demonstrates how large language models can support 30 

evidence-grounded PHI prediction. 31

Bacteriophages are the most abundant biological entities on Earth and are major 33 

drivers of microbial mortality, horizontal gene transfer and microbial community 34 

dynamics [1]. Beyond their ecological roles, phages are increasingly explored as tar-35 

geted antibacterial agents for phage therapy, microbiome engineering and biological 36 

control. A central challenge underlying these applications is phage–host interaction 37 

(PHI) prediction: given a phage genome, which bacterial hosts fall within its host 38 

range? 39 

Existing computational approaches to PHI prediction operate primarily in sequence 40 

space. Sequence-similarity and genome-composition methods infer hosts from exact k-41 

mer matches, similarity scores or composition profiles [2–4], whereas integrated tools 42 

combine heterogeneous signals such as CRISPR spacer matches, tRNA similarity, 43 

prophage evidence and reference-database lookups [5, 6]. Machine-learning approaches 44 

further learn genome-level representations, protein embeddings or graph-based rela-45 

tionships for host prediction [7–10]. These methods have achieved strong performance 46 

at species or genus resolution when reference coverage is sufficient. However, many 47 

of these methods share a common representational premise: PHI-relevant biology is 48 

compressed into numerical features, similarity scores or latent embeddings before pre-49 

diction. This design is powerful for statistical learning, but it makes it difficult to 50 

inspect how distinct biological evidence sources—such as receptor-binding proteins, 51 

host surface structures, defence systems or reference-neighbour associations—support, 52 

contradict or mislead a prediction. This limitation is particularly important because 53 

PHI prediction is not governed by a single type of signal: host associations can rest on 54 

genome-wide similarity, protein homology or shared evolutionary context, but also on 55 

more mechanistic and reference-derived evidence that varies in coverage and reliability 56 

across phage–host pairs. A useful PHI prediction framework should therefore not only 57 

assign a host label, but also expose which supplied evidence makes the assignment 58 

plausible, uncertain or unsupported. 59 

Large language models offer a route for integrating heterogeneous biological evi-60 

dence, and recent systems have linked DNA or protein foundation-model embeddings 61 

with LLMs to support biological inference and generate prediction rationales or rea-62 

soning traces [11, 12]. For PHI prediction, however, this use of LLMs must address two 63 

task-specific requirements. First, host assignment depends on heterogeneous signals 64 

with different coverage and reliability, including sequence similarity, receptor-binding 65 

or adsorption-related evidence, host-surface and defence features, and reference-based 66 

host associations. These signals should remain explicit enough to be inspected and 67 

perturbed, rather than being represented only as latent or aggregated features. Sec-68 

ond, LLM-generated explanations must be grounded in the supplied evidence: missing 69 

receptor, host-surface or defence annotations should not be converted into plausible 70 

but unsupported claims of compatibility. PHI prediction therefore requires a con-71 

strained formulation in which the model integrates explicit evidence records rather 72 

than freely generating biological claims from pretraining knowledge alone. 73 

Here we introduce PHI-Reason , a structured-profile framework that reformulates 74 

PHI prediction as constrained evidence-grounded inference over biological text profiles. 75 

Instead of representing phages and hosts as numerical vectors or latent embeddings, 76

PHI-Reason converts heterogeneous PHI-related information into modular structured 77

A frozen large language model then integrates the supplied records at inference time 81 

to rank hosts over a supplied species-level candidate catalogue or to assess nominated 82 

phage–host pairs. In contrast to conventional supervised classifiers, reference informa-83 

tion is incorporated as visible contextual evidence rather than absorbed into fitted 84 

model parameters. This design makes the evidence interface of prediction inspectable 85 

and perturbable. 86 

We evaluated PHI-Reason at species-level resolution, formulating host prediction as 87 

profile-based ranking over supplied candidate host catalogues. Across RefSeq-derived 88 

and metagenomic proximity-ligation benchmarks, PHI-Reason achieved competi-89 

tive host-prediction performance relative to established sequence- and reference-90 

based methods and recovered complementary correct assignments. More importantly, 91 

because evidence is represented as named text fields, individual evidence layers can 92 

be removed, scrambled or recombined without retraining. We used this property to 93 

test whether predictions were supported by coherent biological evidence or instead 94 

driven by shortcut behaviour such as nearest-neighbour label copying, corrupted ref-95 

erence labels, model-specific idiosyncrasies or unconstrained biological narration. The 96 

same structured-profile representation also supports direct pairwise PHI assessment. 97 

Pairwise scoring evaluates whether a nominated phage–host hypothesis is supported 98 

by the available evidence, complementing host-list ranking when candidate hosts have 99 

already been proposed by database search, sequence similarity, embedding retrieval 100 

or experimental context. We focus the present study on species-level resolution and 101 

discuss the distinct evidence requirements of strain-level prediction separately. 102 

Overall, PHI-Reason illustrates a constrained evidence-integration paradigm for 103 

LLM-based biological prediction. Its contribution is threefold. First, it introduces 104 

a PHI prediction formulation based on explicit biological text profiles rather than 105 

numerical feature compression alone. Second, it provides a perturbable framework for 106 

analysing how LLMs use, compensate for or are misled by different PHI evidence 107 

sources. Third, it makes evidence-interface hallucination risk operationally measur-108 

able by testing whether generated rationales remain grounded in the supplied profile, 109 

while separating rationale faithfulness from prediction correctness. These analyses 110 

characterize when species-level PHI prediction becomes evidence-limited rather than 111 

model-limited. These findings suggest a practical principle for applying LLMs to 112 

annotation-rich biological interaction tasks: language models can integrate explicit 113 

biological evidence and expose when that evidence is insufficient, but they cannot 114 

reliably recover missing compatibility determinants from incomplete or underspecified 115 

profiles. 116

## 2.1 Overview of PHI-Reason 118 

PHI-Reason reformulates phage–host interaction prediction as constrained evidence 119 

integration over genome-derived text profiles, with the language model used as an 120 

inference layer rather than a task-specific host classifier. Instead of compressing phage 121 

and host information into numerical feature vectors or latent embeddings, PHI-Reason 122 

represents infection-relevant evidence as modular natural-language records that pre-123 

serve the identity of distinct biological evidence sources, making the input both 124 

human-readable and experimentally perturbable. 125 

Each phage or host profile is constructed as a modular document composed 126 

of named biological fields. Phage profiles summarize adsorption-related machinery, 127 

gene-level functional annotations, receptor-binding protein evidence and homology-128 

based reference context, including confirmed hosts of nucleotide- and protein-level 129 

homologous reference phages. Host profiles summarize bacterial surface features, 130 

receptor-associated functions, defence repertoires and ecological or isolation-source 131 

information when available. Labelled training data are not used to update model 132 

parameters; instead, related reference phages are retrieved through nucleotide- and 133 

protein-level homology searches, and their known phage–host associations are inserted 134 

into the query phage profile as explicit contextual evidence (Fig.1a). To reduce 135 

identifier- and name-based shortcuts, query phage accessions and phage names were 136 

replaced with opaque hash codes in the base phage profile, and phage identifiers in all 137 

reference-context entries were masked in the same way before inference. 138 

This design separates biological evidence representation from model training. Pro-139 

files remain explicit, updateable and perturbable, whereas the frozen language model 140 

integrates the supplied records at inference time. Individual profile blocks can there-141 

fore be removed, scrambled or recombined without retraining, enabling direct tests of 142 

whether a prediction depends on receptor-binding evidence, nucleotide-level reference 143 

context, protein-level homology, corrupted labels or unsupported free-form reasoning. 144 

PHI-Reason thus predicts hosts while exposing the evidence conditions under which 145 

a prediction is supported, uncertain or misleading. 146 

PHI-Reason supports two complementary inference settings within the same 147 

structured-profile pipeline. In the host-list setting, a query phage is evaluated against 148 

a predefined candidate host catalogue, and the language model ranks all candidates in 149 

a single inference call within a shared evidence context (Fig.1b), providing a controlled 150 

formulation for closed-set species-level host prediction that separates evidence inte-151 

gration from open-ended candidate-host discovery. In the pairwise setting, the same 152 

profile representation is used to evaluate a nominated phage–host pair and assign an 153 

infection-plausibility score (Fig. 1b); this mode is suited to settings in which candidate 154 

hosts have already been proposed by database search, sequence similarity, embedding 155 

retrieval or experimental context. 156 

We organize the study around three questions. First, can PHI-relevant biology rep-157 

resented as structured text profiles support competitive species-level host prediction 158 

without training a task-specific classifier? Second, when a language model makes a PHI 159 

prediction from these profiles, does it integrate multiple supplied evidence sources, or 160

## 2.2 Structured text profiles are sufficient for species-level PHI 168 

## prediction without a task-specific classifier 169 

We use PHI prediction benchmarks to test whether structured biological text pro-170 

files are sufficient to support species-level host prediction and to expose how the 171 

framework integrates evidence, rather than to rank PHI-Reason against existing 172 

tools. Head-to-head accuracy is therefore reported as feasibility evidence under each 173 

method’s native evidence regime, not as a controlled superiority claim. For infer-174 

ence efficiency in species-level benchmarking, we use the host-list inference setting, 175 

in which each query phage is ranked against a predefined benchmark-specific set of 176 

candidate host species in a single inference call. In this setting, query-phage evidence 177 

and candidate-host information are represented as explicit text profiles, and a large 178 

language model, Qwen3-Coder-Next [13] by default, with pretraining data extending 179 

through 30 September 2025, ranks all candidate species jointly. For more detailed 180 

assessment, the same structured-profile framework can also be applied in a pairwise 181 

setting, where each nominated phage–host candidate pair is evaluated separately using 182 

a detailed host-side profile. 183 

We evaluated PHI-Reason on two single-host RefSeq-derived benchmarks and 184 

a metagenomic Hi-C (HiC) benchmark. RefSeq-634 is the held-out partition of 185 

CHERRY [7] (1,306 training / 634 evaluation pairs). VHDB-3150 is drawn from the 186 

larger VHDB collection [14] (1,280 CHERRY-overlapping training / 3,150 evalua-187 

tion pairs). The Hi-C set is the proximity-ligation HiC data of Bignaud et al. [15] 188 

restricted to species-level taxa (52 host species, 406 phages, 46 multi-host), split into 189 

a multi-host-only Sp-1 and a host-number-stratified Sp-2. Phage profiles combined 190 

Prodigal/eggNOG/PHROG base annotations [16–18] with three evidence layers— 191 

RBP homology against 19,081 structure-predicted sequences [19], BLASTN nucleotide 192 

context and BLASTP protein context—with the known hosts of labelled reference 193 

phages inserted as explicit context, without LLM fine-tuning. 194 

We compared against PHIST [2], WIsH [3], PHP [4], DeepHost [10] and 195 

PhaBOX2 [6], retraining the trainable methods and running PhaBOX2 in its updated 196 

ready-to-use configuration, abstentions and out-of-catalogue predictions counted as 197 

errors. We report top-1 accuracy (sp@1) for the RefSeq benchmarks and multi-host 198 

accuracy (MHA-sp) for HiC data. Other evaluation metrics are reported in the Sup-199 

plementary Note. iPHoP [5] predicts and calibrates host assignments at the genus level 200 

only, and deriving a species-level call from its output would require an uncalibrated 201 

post-hoc step the method is not designed for. We therefore compared against it sep-202 

arately at its native genus resolution (Supplementary Table 1a). PHI-Reason reached 203

## 2.3 Evidence perturbation distinguishes grounded inference 255 

## from shortcut use 256 

Beyond competitive species-level prediction performance, a key advantage of repre-257 

senting PHI evidence as structured text is that individual evidence components can 258 

be systematically perturbed without retraining the model. Because evidence is orga-259 

nized into named profile blocks and the model can return field-referenced rationales, 260 

these components can be removed, scrambled or recombined, allowing us to probe 261 

whether predictions depend on the supplied biological evidence or on shortcut-like 262 

cues. We decomposed each phage profile into an annotation-only Base condition and 263 

three layers—RBP evidence, nucleotide-level (BLASTN) and protein-level (BLASTP) 264 

reference context—evaluating each against the same candidate list and prompt and 265 

adding it to Base to estimate its increment over Base under the same prompt and 266 

candidate set. 267 

On RefSeq-634, all three layers carried substantial signal beyond the annotation-268 

only Base profile (sp@1 = 16.9%; Fig. 3a; Supplementary Table 2a): adding BLASTN 269 

raised sp@1 to 53.9% (+37.0 pp), BLASTP to 53.5% (+36.6 pp) and RBP to 40.9% 270 

(+24.0 pp). The combined Full profile was non-additive—its gain did not equal 271 

the sum of the individual layers—consistent with nucleotide-level, protein-level and 272 

receptor-binding evidence capturing overlapping but non-identical host signal. Multi-273 

ple converging layers thus reinforce a prediction, while an intact layer can still carry 274 

it when another is sparse, missing or misleading. 275 

Evidence layers contribute differently across Gram types. The Full profile was most 276 

balanced across envelope types (61.2% overall, 59.8% Gram-positive, 62.2% Gram-277 

negative; Fig. 3b), but individual layers were taxonomically biased. RBP evidence 278 

helped Gram-negative hosts most (+37.4 vs +14.1 pp), pointing to a host-lineage-279 

associated adsorption signal rather than direct strain-level receptor matching, and 280 

mirroring the Gram-negative-dominated RBP database (80.6% of proteins and 73.5% 281 

of source phages, vs 15.5% and 22.4% Gram-positive). In contrast, BLASTN and 282 

BLASTP helped Gram-positive hosts most (+47.0 and +48.1 vs +23.7 and +21.1 283 

pp), indicating that Gram-positive signal was captured more strongly by broader ref-284 

erence context than by RBP alone. Gram-type behaviour thus reflects taxonomically 285 

complementary evidence usage rather than one uniformly dominant signal. 286 

Reference-neighbour availability marks a higher-accuracy, evidence-rich regime. 287 

Using labelled BLASTN-neighbour availability as a coverage split (BLASTP coverage 288 

being near-saturated), the 458 of 634 phages (72.24%) with a neighbour (Group A) 289 

reached sp@1 = 73.6%, versus 29.0% for the 176 (27.8%) without (Group B; −44.6 290 

pp; Fig. 3c, Supplementary Note S1). Group B was not an isolated loss of nucleotide 291 

context but a broadly evidence-poor regime, also showing weaker BLASTP support 292

Label scrambling further shows how this reference dependence should be inter-310 

preted. Scrambling BLASTN labels reduced sp@1 to 50.8%, BLASTP-label scrambling 311 

had a smaller effect (59.3%), and scrambling both blocks reduced performance to 312 

38.0%, below both the RBP-only and label-free controls. Correct labels therefore 313 

strengthen prediction, label removal eliminates much of the reference-derived gain, 314 

and corrupted labels actively mislead the model. At the same time, dual-label scram-315

223-candidate catalogue, indicating that residual RBP, annotation and similarity-317

preted as explicit contextual evidence integrated with other profile fields, rather than 320 

as nearest-neighbour answers copied mechanically from retrieved phages. 321 

Across these perturbations, PHI-Reason used profile evidence conditionally rather 322 

than additively: reference-derived context and receptor-binding evidence each carried 323 

host signal, but the contribution of any one layer depended on whether the others were 324 

available, coherent or corrupted. This behavioural profile—graceful degradation to a 325 

non-retrieval floor, cross-channel compensation and sensitivity to label corruption— 326 

is consistent with labelled reference records acting as explicit, integrable contextual 327 

evidence rather than as deterministic nearest-neighbour lookups. 328

## 2.5 PHI-Reason enables pairwise PHI assessment 383 

PHI-Reason can also operate in a pairwise setting, where each nominated phage–host 384 

pair is evaluated independently rather than ranked against a full candidate list. This 385 

formulation complements the host-list setting used above. Host-list ranking is efficient 386 

for closed-set species-level benchmarking because all candidate hosts are evaluated in a 387 

single LLM inference call, whereas pairwise scoring asks whether an individual phage– 388 

host hypothesis receives sufficient evidence support for downstream prioritization. 389 

On RefSeq-634, each phage was scored against its confirmed host and five 390 

genus-balanced negatives (3,804 pairs per evidence condition) with the same Qwen3-391 

Coder-Next backbone. Because this task is class-imbalanced, PR-AUC is the primary 392 

metric and ROC-AUC is secondary. Scores were well separated (Fig. 5a,c; Supple-393 

mentary Note S6): PR-AUC was 0.926, with F1 = 0.884 and MCC = 0.850 at the 394 

F1-optimal threshold; the confirmed host scored at least as high as all five negatives 395 

for 97.0% of phages (lenient Hits@1 = 0.970); positive and negative pairs had mean 396 

scores of 0.869 and 0.077, respectively (Cohen’s d = 4 .67); 81.5% of pairs scoring ≥0.7 397 

were true positives; and ROC-AUC was 0.984 (95% CI 0.978–0.989). These metrics 398 

should be interpreted in light of the 1:5 candidate ratio used here. As the Listwise-6 399 

control below shows, reducing candidate density substantially inflates apparent per-400 

formance, so pairwise scoring is most appropriate when candidate hosts have already 401 

been narrowed by upstream retrieval. A Listwise-6 control showed that the apparent 402 

pairwise advantage was mainly a candidate-density effect rather than a benefit of the 403 

pairwise formulation itself. When the host-list prompt was restricted to the same six 404 

candidates used in the pairwise task, listwise and pairwise performance were nearly 405 

identical (Fig. 5b). Pairwise reliability also depended on the same specialised evi-406 

dence as host-list ranking: removing specialised evidence weakened score separation 407 

and high-confidence precision, whereas scrambling both BLAST label sources pushed 408 

performance toward the RBP-only regime (Fig. 5c,d). Pairwise assessment is therefore 409 

best viewed as a complementary mode for candidate-narrowed ranking and triage. It 410 

provides a continuous evidence-support score, not an absolutely calibrated probability, 411 

and preserves the same evidence dependencies observed in host-list inference. 412

A central risk in applying large language models to scientific inference is that a gener-415

a species-level ranked host prediction, allowing both to be checked against the exact 419 

supplied evidence. Because hallucination has no universal operational definition, and 420

# 3 Discussion 513 

PHI-Reason reframes phage–host interaction prediction as structured biological evi-514 

dence integration rather than task-specific host classification. Instead of mapping 515 

genomes directly to host labels through numerical features, PHI-Reason converts 516 

phage and host genomes into modular textual profiles in which receptor-binding evi-517 

dence, nucleotide-level reference context, protein-level homology, host surface features 518 

and defence-related information are represented as explicit biological records. A gen-519 

eral language model without fine-tuning then acts as an inference layer over these 520 

records, comparing and integrating heterogeneous evidence at prediction time. 521 

A key distinction of PHI-Reason is that it preserves the evidence interface of 522 

biological inference. Recent language-model applications in bioinformatics have often 523 

treated biological sequences as language-like objects, using protein language mod-524 

els or DNA foundation models to learn latent sequence representations. More recent 525 

hybrid systems further connect these representations to LLM-based biological reason-526 

ing, such as BioReason [11] and BioReason-Pro [12]. A related line of work has also 527 

begun to translate biological relationships into language-model reasoning tasks. For 528 

example, ProLLM formulates protein–protein interaction prediction as a text-to-text 529 

problem by representing multi-step protein signalling pathways as Protein Chain-of-530 

Thought prompts [29]. PHI-Reason differs in the structure of the prediction problem 531 

it addresses. Phage–host interaction prediction requires integrating heterogeneous evi-532 

dence across biological scales, including receptor-binding evidence, reference-neighbour 533 

host labels, nucleotide- and protein-level homology, host surface features and defence-534 

related information. Rather than adapting an LLM to reason over latent sequence 535 

representations or pathway-style protein relation chains, PHI-Reason renders these dis-536 

tinct evidence sources as structured textual profiles and uses a frozen LLM to integrate 537 

them at inference time. By keeping the signals as named, inspectable and perturbable 538 

fields, PHI-Reason allows evidence layers to be removed, scrambled or recombined 539 

without retraining, making it possible to evaluate not only whether a host prediction is 540 

correct, but also which evidence conditions make the prediction supported, uncertain 541 

or misleading. 542 

This perturbable representation also makes PHI-Reason an experimental plat-543 

form for testing evidence descriptions for species-level PHI prediction. By removing, 544 

isolating or corrupting individual profile layers while holding the model, prompt 545 

and candidate catalogue fixed, the framework can assess evidence utility directly 546 

rather than only through aggregate method performance. The strongest overall sig-547 

nal was homology-derived reference context—nucleotide- and protein-level homologues 548 

together with the known hosts of related phages—while RBP evidence provided 549 

complementary support, especially for Gram-negative hosts. This is consistent with 550 

why existing PHI tools such as PhaBOX2 and iPHoP combine sequence similarity, 551 

reference-database, CRISPR/prophage, composition or related-virus signals in differ-552 

ent forms: these evidence types reflect how current PHI knowledge is organized and 553 

carry substantial host-discriminative information. PHI-Reason makes this principle 554 

explicit and perturbable, allowing useful homology and adsorption-related signals to 555 

be distinguished from misleading neighbours or corrupted labels and recombined with 556 

annotation and host-profile fields. More broadly, the same platform can rapidly test 557

The same evidence interface reframes hallucination in PHI inference as an 563

or receptor class, allowing the model to match supplied evidence to named biologi-593

machinery of a particular phage matches the surface architecture of a particular iso-595

related strains can share more than 99% of their genome while differing at surface and 597 

defence loci that determine susceptibility, so similarity signals informative at species 598 

level can become uninformative or misleading within a species. 599 

This shift is consequential for a language-model-based inference framework, 600

Overall, PHI-Reason illustrates a profile-based evidence-integration paradigm for 623 

biological interaction prediction, in which heterogeneous evidence is organized into 624 

modular, human-readable and perturbable text profiles and integrated by a frozen 625

field-referenced, it reframes the language model as an inspectable evidence integrator 638

# 4 Methods 643 

## 4.1 Profile generation 644 

PHI-Reason represents phage genomes and candidate host genomes as structured 645 

textual profiles. Profiles were generated before benchmark inference using deter-646 

ministic bioinformatics pipelines, curated lookup tables and predefined formatting 647 

rules. No language model was used during profile construction. Each phage pro-648 

file contained gene-level functional annotations, sequence-derived protein descriptors, 649 

receptor-binding-protein evidence and optional nucleotide- and protein-level reference-650 

context blocks. Each host profile contained surface-relevant proteins, anti-phage 651 

defence systems [30] and curated receptor, host-range and species-discriminating infor-652 

mation. These fields were represented as named biological records so that they could be 653 

retained, removed, scrambled or recombined in downstream perturbation experiments. 654 

4.1.1 Phage genome profiles 655 

For phage profiles, open reading frames were predicted from each genome using 656 

Prodigal v2.6.3 and annotated with complementary phage and orthology resources, 657 

including PHROG v4 [18] searched with MMseqs2 13-45111+ds-2 [31], and eggNOG-658 

mapper v2.1.13 [17] using the eggNOG v5.0.2 [32] database. PHROG product names 659 

were used as the primary standardized labels when available, with non-redundant 660 

eggNOG descriptions retained as qualifiers. For each annotated protein, sequence-661 

derived descriptors including length, theoretical isoelectric point, molecular weight, 662 

hydrophobicity and an approximate transmembrane-helix count were computed and 663 

stored as profile fields. Annotated genes were grouped into functional sections relevant 664 

to host inference, including tail and host adsorption, lysis, head and DNA packaging, 665 

connector and head–tail junction, lysogeny, replication and metabolism, gene expres-666 

sion and regulation, and accessory host-derived factors. Genes without functional 667 

annotation were summarized as counts to retain information about hypothetical-gene 668 

burden without exceeding the inference context budget. 669 

4.1.2 Receptor-binding evidence 670 

Receptor-binding evidence was generated by searching tail-associated or adsorption-671 

related phage proteins against PHIStruct [19], a curated receptor-binding-protein 672 

corpus comprising 19,081 phage receptor-binding protein sequences across 238 host 673 

genera. The reference FASTA was indexed with DIAMOND v2.1.24 and queried using 674 

proteins annotated as tail-associated by PHROG or containing adsorption-related 675 

product keywords, including tail fibre, tail spike and receptor-binding protein. Hits 676 

were filtered by sequence identity, query coverage and e-value thresholds, and the top 677 

hits for each query protein were aggregated by host genus. 678 

For each query gene–host genus pair, the profile retained the best sequence identity, 679 

best query coverage and number of hits. The top host genera were then appended to 680 

the corresponding tail-gene entry in the phage profile. This evidence layer therefore 681 

provided coarse adsorption-related host-genus signals, rather than direct protein-to-682 

host-species labels. In particular, PHIStruct-derived RBP evidence did not assign a 683

## 4.2 Language-model inference and computational environment 737 

PHI-Reason in species level experiments used Qwen3-Coder-Next:Q4 K M served 738 

locally with Ollama v0.23.2 [35]. Inference was performed with temperature 0.1, a 739 

32,768-token context window and non-streaming generation. Unless otherwise stated, 740 

experiments used the no-thinking prompt variant; thinking-mode runs removed the 741 

/no think prefix and used a larger generation budget. Outputs were required to follow 742 

predefined JSON schemas and were retried once if parsing failed. Successfully parsed 743 

phage-level or pair-level outputs were cached and reused in downstream analyses. 744 

Two prompt formats were used. In the host-list setting, one inference call was made 745 

per query phage using the compressed 223-host catalogue, the query phage profile 746 

and fixed scoring instructions; the model returned the top 30 ranked candidate hosts. 747 

Hosts not returned in the top-30 output were assigned an implicit score of zero. In the 748 

pairwise setting, one inference call was made per nominated phage–host pair using one 749

## 4.3 Evaluation benchmarks 776 

Benchmark datasets 777 

We evaluated PHI-Reason on three datasets that differ in curation procedure, host-778 

label structure and biological domain: RefSeq-634, VHDB-3150 and a metagenomic 779 

HiC proximity-ligation benchmark. 780 

RefSeq-634 RefSeq-634 is the held-out evaluation partition of the CHERRY-781 

1940 dataset [7], which contains 1,940 phage–host pairs assembled from NCBI RefSeq 782 

[39] with species-level host assignments across 223 bacterial taxa. We partitioned this 783 

dataset by phage accession into a 1,306-phage reference partition and a 634-phage 784 

held-out evaluation partition. The 1,306-phage partition was used only as an explicit 785 

reference corpus for nucleotide- and protein-level context retrieval. 786 

VHDB-3150 VHDB [14] is a larger RefSeq-derived benchmark comprising 4,698 787 

phage–host pairs across 498 bacterial host species. To construct a benchmark con-788 

sistent with the RefSeq-634 reference split, we used the 1,280 VHDB phages that 789 

overlapped with the CHERRY-1306 training partition as the training/reference par-790 

tition. These phages were used as the labelled reference corpus for nucleotide- and 791 

protein-level context retrieval and for retraining applicable baseline methods. The 792 

remaining VHDB test phages were used for evaluation. Among these test phages, 793 

268 did not have species-level ground truth suitable for species-level evaluation, leav-794 

ing 3,150 phages with confirmed species-level host labels; this subset is referred to as 795 

VHDB-3150. 796 

HiC The HiC benchmark is derived from MetaHiC proximity-ligation co-797 

sequencing of human gut metagenomes. Viral contigs are linked to candidate host 798 

metagenome-assembled genomes (MAGs) through physical co-localisation evidence; 799 

MAG-level associations are then collapsed to species level using GTDB taxonomy, 800 

producing species-level phage–host associations across a 52-species host universe H.801 

Unlike the isolate benchmarks, a single phage contig may be associated with multi-802 

ple host species; therefore, the ground truth for each phage q is a set of host species 803 

Yq ⊆ H rather than a single label. We followed the evaluation protocol of [15], report-804 

ing results on two complementary splits: Sp-1 ( n = 46 multi-host test phages) and Sp-2 805 

(n = 82 host-number-stratified test phages). This benchmark provides a domain-shift 806 

evaluation because the candidate hosts are gut metagenomic species represented by 807 

selected MAG-derived reference genomes rather than RefSeq-derived isolate genomes. 808

Abstention-as-error policy 809 

Unless otherwise stated, all evaluations used an abstention-as-error policy. If a method 810 

produced no prediction, returned a host outside the candidate space, or failed to 811 

assign a species-level host under the required output resolution, the case was counted 812 

as an incorrect prediction. This policy keeps denominators fixed across methods and 813 

prevents apparent performance gains from selective abstention on difficult cases. 814 

## 4.4 Baseline methods 815 

We compared PHI-Reason with five representative phage–host prediction meth-816 

ods: PHP, PhaBOX2, PHIST, WIsH and DeepHost. Together, these baselines cover 817 

composition-based probabilistic models, exact k-mer matching, hybrid multi-signal 818 

reference-based pipelines and supervised sequence models. Baseline methods were 819 

evaluated on a benchmark when their training requirements and output format were 820 

compatible with the corresponding evaluation task. For each benchmark, baseline pre-821 

dictions were mapped to the same benchmark-specific candidate host space used for 822 

PHI-Reason whenever possible. Predictions assigned to hosts outside the candidate 823 

set were counted as incorrect. Queries for which a method returned no host prediction 824 

were treated as abstentions and counted as errors. 825 

PHP is a host prediction method based on k-mer composition profiles and Gaus-826 

sian mixture modelling. Query phages and candidate hosts were represented by k-mer 827 

frequency features, and candidate hosts were ranked according to the likelihood 828 

assigned by the fitted host models. PHP was rebuilt separately for each benchmark 829 

using the corresponding training partition and candidate host set. For RefSeq-634, 830 

PHP was trained using the CHERRY-1306 training partition. For VHDB-3150, PHP 831 

was trained using the VHDB training partition defined by overlap with the CHERRY 832 

training set. For the HiC benchmark, PHP was retrained using the corresponding 833 

training split and the 52 species-level candidate hosts, with each host species repre-834 

sented by one selected MAG-derived reference genome. Because PHP assigns scores 835 

to all candidate hosts, it produced a complete host ranking for every query phage. 836 

PhaBOX2 is a hybrid multi-signal phage analysis framework that combines host 837 

prediction evidence from sequence-based comparisons, k-mer features and reference 838 

databases. We ran PhaBOX2 v2.2 using its complete default database for the RefSeq-839 

634 and VHDB-3150 isolate-derived benchmarks. Reported host assignments were 840 

parsed, taxonomic prefixes were removed where present, and the resulting host names 841 

were normalized to the benchmark taxonomy before species-level evaluation. Pre-842 

dictions assigned to hosts outside the benchmark-specific candidate host space were 843 

counted as incorrect. Because PhaBOX2 returns sparse host assignments rather than 844 

a calibrated score or complete ranking over all candidate hosts, it was evaluated 845 

using its top-ranked species-level prediction. PhaBOX2 was not evaluated on the HiC 846 

benchmark because the HiC task required multi-host ranking over 52 species-level 847 

candidate hosts, each represented by one selected MAG-derived reference genome, 848 

whereas PhaBOX2 returns conservative sparse host assignments rather than complete 849 

candidate-host rankings. 850 

PHIST is an exact k-mer matching method that identifies statistically signifi-851 

cant sequence similarity between query phage genomes and candidate host genomes. 852

We ran PHIST with default parameters against the benchmark-specific host genome 853

WIsH is a host prediction method based on homogeneous Markov models of host 858 

nucleotide composition. For each benchmark, host-specific Markov models were built 859 

from the corresponding candidate host genomes using the WIsH build procedure. 860 

Query phage genomes were then scored against all candidate host models, and hosts 861

the HiC benchmark using the corresponding benchmark-specific candidate host sets. 863 

Because WIsH assigns a likelihood to each candidate host, it produced a complete 864 

ranking for every query phage. 865 

DeepHost is a supervised deep learning method that predicts host labels from 866 

one-hot encoded phage genome sequences using a convolutional neural network. As a 867 

closed-set classifier, DeepHost can only predict host classes observed during training. 868 

We therefore evaluated DeepHost only in settings where a compatible benchmark-869 

specific host vocabulary could be defined for both training and testing. DeepHost was 870 

not evaluated on the RefSeq-derived isolate benchmarks because the host classes in 871 

the training and test partitions were not identical, leaving some test hosts outside 872 

the model’s prediction space. For the HiC benchmark, DeepHost was retrained on the 873 

training split using the 52 species-level host labels and evaluated on the corresponding 874 

held-out test split. 875 

## 4.5 Evidence perturbation experiments 876 

We used profile-level perturbations to test how individual evidence layers contributed 877 

to PHI-Reason predictions without changing the language-model backbone, prompt 878 

format, candidate host list or evaluation metric. All perturbation experiments were 879 

performed by modifying named fields in the input profiles before inference. No model 880 

parameters were updated, and each perturbation variant was evaluated with the same 881 

inference backend and decoding settings as the corresponding full-profile condition. 882 

For species-level host-list experiments, the Base condition was defined as an 883 

annotation-only phage profile. It retained query-genome-derived functional annota-884 

tions, gene-level product labels, protein descriptors and taxonomy-derived annotation 885 

signals, but removed receptor-binding evidence, nucleotide-level reference context and 886 

protein-level reference context. Single-layer conditions were generated by adding one 887 

specialised evidence layer back to the Base profile: receptor-binding evidence only, 888 

BLASTN-derived nucleotide-level reference context only, or BLASTP-derived protein-889 

level reference context only. The Full condition contained all three specialised evidence 890 

layers. Independent layer gains were measured relative to the Base condition, and 891 

redundancy was quantified by comparing the observed Full-profile gain with the sum 892 

of the single-layer gains. 893

## 4.7 Quantifying evidence-interface hallucination risk 956 

Scope and definitions 957 

We evaluated the frozen predictor ( Qwen-Coder-Next:Q4 K M ) in its full-evidence con-958 

figuration on RefSeq-634, analysing for each phage the cached full-evidence inference 959 

output: the generated rationale, the ranked host output and the verbatim input pro-960 

file. Because hallucination has no universal operational definition and prediction error 961 

alone is insufficient to identify it, we operationalised three related evidence-interface 962 

quantities, evaluating only the evidence available to the model rather than the exter-963 

nal biological literature. Profile faithfulness is a rationale-level quantity: the fraction 964 

of rationale claims entailed by the input profile, with a claim considered unsup-965 

ported when no profile field entails it. Rationale-linked hallucination is an incorrect 966 

species-level prediction whose rationale contains at least one claim not grounded in 967 

the supplied profile; this was operationalised as an unsupported field-referenced claim 968 

for the programmatic verifier, and as a contradicted or unverifiable semantic claim 969 

for the blind judge. Answer-level hallucination is the stricter case of an incorrect pre-970 

diction whose predicted host genus appears nowhere in the host-supporting evidence 971 

fields of the supplied profile. Because all candidate hosts are visible in the closed-set 972 

ranking task, catalogue membership alone was not counted as supporting evidence. 973 

Prediction correctness was scored at the host-species level after whitespace/underscore 974 

normalisation, whereas answer-level hallucination and the error-source decomposition 975 

were evaluated at the host-genus level, the resolution at which reference-neighbour 976 

labels, BLASTN/BLASTP neighbour hosts and RBP signals are most consistently 977 

reported. Blind atomic-claim judging was applied to all 634 phages with two judge 978 

models; resampling and reliability benchmarking used a shared correctness-balanced 979 

200-phage sample, and the held-out natural subset ( n = 434) was its complement 980 

within RefSeq-634. 981

All statistical analyses were performed on fixed model outputs without model re-1038

fixed under the abstention-as-error policy. Confidence intervals for species-level met-1040

null. 1052

Fig. 1 Overview of the PHI-Reason framework. a , Phage and bacterial genomes are con-verted into structured textual profiles using deterministic genome-annotation pipelines.
