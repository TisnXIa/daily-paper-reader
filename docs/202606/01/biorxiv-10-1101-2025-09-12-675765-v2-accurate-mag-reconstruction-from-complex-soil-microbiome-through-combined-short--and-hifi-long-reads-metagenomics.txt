# Abstract 15 

Background 16 

Advances in high-fidelity long-read (HiFi-LR) sequencing technologies have opened new opportunities to 17 

explore the microbial genomic diversity of complex environments, such as soils. While short-read (SR) 18 

sequencing has enabled broad insights at the gene level, the limited read length constrains the reconstruction 19 

of complete genomes. HiFi-LRs, in contrast, improve assembly continuity and completeness, supporting 20 

higher-resolution taxonomic and functional annotation. However, the cost and relatively low throughput of 21 

HiFi-LR sequencing can limit genome recovery—particularly at the binning stage, where coverage depth is 22 

critical. In this study, we assess the benefit of combining HiFi-LR and SR sequencing for genome-resolved 23 

characterization of a soil microbiome. 24 

Results 25 

We generated metagenomic data for a tunnel-cultivated soil sample using high coverage Illumina SRs as well 26 

as a combination of two HiFi-LR sequencing platforms (PacBio Sequel II and PacBio Revio). We found 27 

that assemblies generated from pooled HiFi-LR data alone exhibited higher completeness compared to those 28 

from ultra-deep SR data. Incorporating SR-derived coverage information for the binning of HiFi-LR contigs 29 

further increased both the number and quality of recovered metagenome-assembled genomes (MAGs), with 30 

a 24% increase in MAG recovery (313 vs. 252) and lower contamination levels (116 vs. 132 contaminated 31 

bins; mean 7.09 vs. 8.07), compared to using HiFi-LR data alone. This approach enabled the recovery of 32 

61 additional MAGs, including 67% of low-abundance and taxonomically diverse lineages such as Archaea, 33 

representing 36 novel lineages. 34 

Conclusion 35 

Our results demonstrate that integrating HiFi-LR and SR sequencing markedly enhances genome recovery 36 

and binning accuracy in a highly diverse environment such as soil. The hybrid approach employed leverages 37 

the strengths of both technologies, leading to more contiguous assemblies and enabling the recovery of a 38 

broader range of genomes, including low-abundance and taxonomically diverse taxa. While factors such as 39 

sequencing depth, cost, and DNA quality remain important considerations, our study provides practical 40 

guidance for designing future soil metagenomics projects and underscores the value of adopting long-read 41 

technologies for more comprehensive characterization of complex microbial communities. 42

# Background 45 

Soil is one of the most diverse microbial ecosystems and remains underexplored [ 1 , 2]. It hosts an extraordinary 46 

diversity of microorganisms, including bacteria, archaea, fungi, protists, and microscopic animals. The work 47 

of Torsvik et al. [ 3 ] reports that a single gram of soil may contain up to 10 10 microbial cells covering diverse 48 

taxa, and may harbour up to thousands of different species. Compared to host-associated biomes such as 49 

the human gut, soil microbiomes exhibit much greater alpha-diversity and a higher proportion of unknown 50 

taxa. Remarkably, just ∼2% of bacterial phylotypes account for nearly 50% of all bacterial sequences in soils 51 

worldwide [ 4]. Comprehensive characterization of soil microbiomes, especially rare species, is essential for 52 

advancing our understanding of biogeochemical cycles [ 5 ] and developing sustainable agricultural practices 53 

[6]. Microbial genomes encode the genes responsible for the metabolic capacities and ecological functions that 54 

shape soil processes, including interactions, catalysis of biochemical reactions, and contributions to ecosystem 55 

functioning [ 7]. Thus, uncovering the diversity of soil microbial genomes is crucial for harnessing microbial 56 

resources and promoting soil health and sustainability [6]. 57 

Various approaches have been developed to characterise soil microbial communities. Although culturomics 58 

has provided valuable insights, many soil species remain uncultivable under laboratory conditions [ 8]. 59 

Consequently, metagenomics and other culture-independent omics approaches have become the primary 60 

strategies for exploring the taxonomic diversity and functional potential of soil microbiomes. Shotgun 61 

metagenomics allows direct DNA sequencing from complex microbial communities without prior cultivation. 62 

This approach can provide a genomic snapshot of the microbial community in a given soil sample, even if 63 

dark matter, constituted by rare species, remains difficult to capture. Metagenomics has revolutionised our 64 

ability to investigate the diversity and functions of uncultured organisms, providing unprecedented insights 65 

into the genetic diversity, functional potential, and ecological roles of microbiota [ 9 ]. De novo metagenomic 66 

assembly allows the reconstruction of genomes species, including uncultured taxa, and the prediction of the 67 

encoded proteins [ 10, 11 ]. However, the reconstruction of high-quality genomes from the complex mixture of 68 

sequenced reads remains very limited in soil, due to the extremely high microbial diversity, uneven species 69 

abundance, and the presence of closely related strains, which together complicate assembly and binning 70 

processes. Consequently, only a few whole genomes are usually obtained, with many unassembled reads [ 12]. 71 

Most metagenomic projects conducted over the past decade have relied on short-read (SR) sequencing 72 

technologies, generating high-throughput datasets typically composed of 2x 50–150-base-pair fragments. 73 

Consequently, SR-based metagenomic studies still dominate public repositories such as the JGI IMG/M 74 

[13 ] and EBI MGnify [ 14 ] databases in terms of available sequence data. Despite their high accuracy and 75 

throughput, SR remains limited to face the challenge of reconstructing genomes containing multicopy genes 76 

or repeat-rich regions, which are inherently difficult to resolve with short sequences, creating ambiguous 77 

paths in the assembly graph and leading to fragmented assemblies [ 15]. In the 2022 release of the JGI 78 

IMG/M platform, only 10% of SR-derived contigs exceeded one kilobase in length, illustrating the extent of 79 

assembly fragmentation. Moreover, inconsistencies in protein taxonomic assignments within contigs suggest 80 

the presence of chimeric assemblies [11]. 81 

Increased read length in metagenomic studies enhances contig lengths by providing long-range information, 82 

which allows better resolution of repetitive sequences. In recent years, Oxford Nanopore Technologies (ONT) 83 

[16 , 17 ] and Pacific Biosciences (PacBio) [ 18 ] platforms have significantly improved read accuracy, length, 84 

and throughput. While the first generations of LR sequencing technologies were characterised by high error 85 

rates, which led to the recommendation of combining them with SR for error correction during the assembly 86 

step [ 19 ], recent technologies provide high-fidelity (HiFi) sequences, with error rates that are similarly low to 87 

those of SR [ 20 , 21 ]. To leverage the complementary strengths of SR and LR technologies, several hybrid 88 

metagenomic strategies have been developed. For assembly, tools such as OPERA-MS [ 16 ] use LR data 89 

primarily to scaffold contigs initially assembled from SR reads. More recently, HyLight integrated HiFi-LR 90 

and SR from low-depth metagenomes [ 22] and MetaPlatanus [ 23 ] implemented mutual-support strategies or 91 

overlap-graph approaches to achieve strain-level resolution. Integrated workflows such as MUFFIN [ 24 ] further 92 

combine hybrid assembly with differential coverage binning to recover high-quality metagenome-assembled 93 

genomes (MAGs). Recent benchmarking studies additionally demonstrated that multi-sample hybrid binning 94 

strategies outperform other approaches for recovering near-complete strains and detecting antimicrobial 95 

resistance genes [25]. 96

Although several studies have explored hybrid approaches for metagenomic assembly and binning, a strategy 97 

relying exclusively on LR data for assembly while using SR data exclusively for binning remains unexplored. 98 

In this study, we assessed the added value of HiFi-LR sequencing compared to SR sequencing for metagenomic 99 

assembly in complex environments such as soil. We generated and analysed PacBio HiFi-LRs, Illumina 100 

SRs, and metabarcoding data from a cultivated soil sample, comparing contigs and MAG recovery for each 101 

individual technology and for both combined. Our results demonstrate that a hybrid strategy, combining the 102 

higher contiguity of HiFi-LR assemblies with the greater sequencing depth of SR data, improves genome 103 

reconstruction. Incorporating SR-derived coverage into HiFi-LR binning led to an increase in MAG recovery (+ 104 

24%), and reduced the global contamination levels compared to HiFi-LR data alone. The gain is particularly 105 

marked for low-abundance taxa. Comparison with microbial diversity estimates from metabarcoding data 106 

confirmed that this hybrid approach captures a broader spectrum of the soil microbiome. 107 

# Methods 108 

## Sampling 109 

We collected a soil sample between two rows of salad crops in a tunnel farming setup in Lambesc, southern 110 

France. Rapid freezing, followed by subsequent freeze-drying procedures at -40 °C, immediately preserved 111 

the soil sample. These preservation and preparation steps were meticulously executed at the Conservatoire 112 

de Ressources Génétiques (CRG), hosted within the Genosol platform in Dijon, France, to maintain the 113 

molecular integrity of the samples in readiness for subsequent analytical procedures. 114 

## DNA extraction 115 

Total genomic DNA was extracted using modified IS0-11063 protocols for DNA extraction and Nucleospin soil 116 

for DNA purification [ 26 ]. From 1g of dry equivalent soil (freeze-dried), DNA was extracted by mechanical 117 

lysis using FastPrep 5G (MP Bio) coupled with cell lysis with denaturing detergent (lysis buffer: EDTA, 118 

Tris pH 8.0, NaCl and SDS 2%). Deproteinisation consisted of precipitating the proteins with a high salt 119 

concentration (KAc 5M) followed by high-speed centrifugation to separate the DNA (supernatant) and 120 

proteins (pellet). DNA has been precipitated with isopropanol (v/v) and washed with ethanol 70%. DNA was 121 

quantified with NanoDrop technology with a concentration of 114.7 ng/ μL (260/280 ratio of 1.84, 260/230 122 

ratio of 1.5). As Nanodrop could overestimate the DNA content, the QuBit method, a more reliable method 123 

for DNA quantification, was used and yielded a dosage of 50.5 ng/ μL (total DNA quantity 2.62 μg in a 124 

volume of ca. 50 μL). Notably, the sample exhibited a straightforward appearance, denoting its suitability for 125 

further investigation. 126 

## Metagenomic and metabarcoding sequencing and raw data processing 127 

Long-read metagenomic sequencing 128 

Extracted soil DNA was sequenced on the PacBio platforms using the HiFi sequencing mode. One complete 129 

SMRT cell was run on both the Sequel II and Revio platforms, generating two datasets. For the Sequel II 130 

sequencing, high-molecular-weight DNA with an average fragment size of ~8 Kb was used to prepare an 131 

unamplified, non-multiplexed HiFi library with the Template Prep Kit v2.0, following the PacBio metagenomic 132 

shotgun protocol. Sequencing was carried out on a SMRT Cell 8M over 30 h. For the Revio sequencing, 133 

the HiFi library was prepared with ~8 Kb not-sheared native DNA, using the Template Prep Kit 3.0. The 134 

SMRTBell library was sequenced on a SMRTCell 25M over 24 h with the Revio sequencing plate reagents. 135 

For both Sequel II and Revio datasets, adapter removal, read quality filtering (minimum Q20), and generation 136 

of circular consensus sequencing (CCS) reads were performed using dedicated PacBio pipelines. HiFi-LR 137 

reads of both platforms were pooled to increase sequencing depth. 138 

Short-read metagenomic sequencing 139 

DNA from the same soil sample underwent a second extraction and was sequenced at ultra-deep coverage on 140 

a full NextSeq 2000 Illumina P3 flow cell. 141

16S rRNA gene amplicon sequencing 142 

16S rRNA gene amplicon sequencing data were generated from the same soil sample through targeted 143 

sequencing of the V3-V4 region of the 16S rRNA gene using primers 341F_UDI and 785R_UDI on the 144 

Illumina NextSeq 2000 platform, producing 2x300bp reads. Library preparation and sequencing were 145 

conducted with the protocol described by [ 27 ]. Based on the selected primers, we expected to sequence 146 

approximately 444 bp of the 16S rRNA gene, with an expected overlap of approximately 156 bp between 147 

R1 and R2. Paired-end reads were merged using FLASH v0.23.4 [ 28 ] with flexible overlap settings (--148 

min-overlap 10, --max-overlap 301) to reconstruct full-length amplicons, even with region size variability. 149 

Primer sequences were trimmed using Cutadapt v4.9 [ 29] with parameters optimised for mismatch handling 150 

(--error-rate 0.1, --match-read-wildcards, --overlap). Quality filtering was performed using Fastp v0.23.4 151 

[30], with the following parameters: a Phred quality score threshold of 30 (--qualified_quality_phred 30), a 152 

minimum sequence length of 230 bp (--length_required 230), and an average quality of 30 (--average_qual). 153 

Additional trimming at the 5’ and 3’ ends was done using a sliding window approach (--cut_window_size 154 

4, --cut_mean_quality 30) with low-quality bases removed (--cut_front, --cut_right). Adaptor trimming 155 

was disabled (--disable_adapter_trimming) to avoid interference with prior primer removal steps. Chimeric 156 

sequences were detected and removed using Vsearch v2.9.1 [31] in de novo mode (--uchime3_denovo). 157 

## Bioinformatic analyses 158 

A summary of the bioinformatic pipeline and metagenomic processing is available in Supp. Fig. 1. After 159 

data acquisition, we performed quality control on each dataset using FastQC software version 0.12.0 [ 32] with 160 

default parameters. The results were merged using MultiQC software version 1.25.1 [33] in default mode. 161 

Assembly 162 

Paired-end metagenomic SR were assembled using MEGAHIT v1.2.9 [ 34 ] with the ‘--presets meta-large’ 163 

parameter. HiFi-LR were assembled using MetaMDBG v.0.3 with default parameters [35]. 164 

Binning 165 

We used three binning strategies according to the sequencing data used to perform alignments against contigs 166 

(Supp. Fig. 1). Sequence features, such as GC content, tetranucleotide frequency, and coverage as a proxy 167 

for abundance, were used for clustering contigs that are supposed to belong to the same organism in the bin, 168 

toward the reconstruction of MAGs. 169 

• Binning strategy 1: short-read assembly with short read mapping for contig binning (SR-SR). 170 

• Binning strategy 2: long-read assembly with long read mapping for contig binning (LR-LR). 171 

• Binning strategy 3: long-read assembly with short read mapping for contig binning (Hybrid SR-LR 172 

strategy). 173 

In binning strategies 1 and 3, short reads were mapped on contigs using BWA-MEM2 v.2.2 [ 36 ] with default 174 

parameters. In Binning strategy 2, long reads were mapped with Minimap2 v.2.26 [ 37 ] with the parameters 175 

-x map-hifi -a --sam-hit-only --secondary=no. 176 

In all the binning strategies, resulting alignments were used to compute contig coverage, which served as a 177 

proxy for estimating species abundance. Mapping outputs were processed using Samtools v.1.10 [ 38 ]. Then, 178 

in all three strategies, contigs were binned using MetaBAT2 v.2.15 [ 39 ] in default mode, and SemiBin v.1.5.0 179 

[40] with parameters ‘--environment soil’ and ’--sequencing-type=long_read’ for long-read processing (Binning 180 

strategies 2 and 3). The obtained results were merged and dereplicated using DAStool v.1.1.6 [41]. 181 

Bin and Metagenome-aAssembled Genome (MAG) evaluation 182 

Gene prediction was performed using Prodigal v2.6.3 [ 42 ], and the completeness and contamination of bins 183 

were assessed using CheckM2 v1.0.1 [ 43 ]. Those analyses were performed using a previously published 184 

workflow [ 44 ]. We required at least 50% completeness and less than 10% contamination, without any filter on 185

To evaluate the impact of short-read sequencing depth on contig binning efficiency, additional subsampling 195

Taxonomic classification 201 

To characterise the taxonomic composition of MAGs, contigs, and reads from each dataset, two complementary 202 

taxonomic assignment strategies were applied. For MAGs with more than 50% completeness, taxonomic 203 

classification was confirmed using GTDB-tk v.2.1.1 [ 48 ] with database version 214, providing robust information 204 

based on multigene phylogenetic placement [49]. 205 

Additionally, both Illumina 16S rRNA amplicon sequences and PacBio metagenomic contigs as well as reads 206 

underwent a common taxonomic assignment strategy to facilitate their comparison. Reads (metabarcoding 207 

and LR metagenomics) and contigs (LR and SR metagenomics) were assigned a taxon using MMseqs2 [ 50 ]208 

“easy-search” module against the SILVA 138.2 SSURef NR99 database of curated 16S rRNA sequences as 209 

the reference. To ensure high-confidence assignments, homology searches were performed using a stringent 210 

threshold of 97% sequence identity and a minimum alignment length of 100 bp (parameters --min-seq-id 0.97 211 

and --min-aln-len 100, respectively). Coverage was set to improve sensitivity in detecting partial sequence 212 

matches of fragmented SR and LR reads and contigs by including overlapping hits (--cov-mode 2). Alignment 213 

coverage was analysed using BEDtools [ 51 ], calculating the proportion of each reference sequence covered by 214 

aligned reads. Species with 16S sequences from SILVA that were covered on more than 97% of their length 215 

were retained for further study. Eukaryotic hits against the SILVA database [ 52 ] were excluded to ensure 216 

consistency in taxonomic inference. The taxonomic lineage was used to increment counts in an abundance 217 

matrix for each taxonomic rank from species to domain. These matrices underwent downstream diversity 218 

analyses, which were performed using custom Jupyter Notebooks. Phylogenetic visualisations were generated 219 

using the Interactive Tree Of Life (iTOL) v7 [53]. 220 

# Results 221 

## HiFi-LR sequencing delivers more comprehensive and continuous metagenome 222 

## reconstruction. 223 

To compare shotgun metagenomic assembly performance of short-read and long-read sequencing technologies, 224 

we sequenced a soil sample with Illumina SR and PacBio HiFi-LR (Sequel II and Revio) platforms and 225 

assembled the resulting datasets. Our data show that HiFi-LR sequencing, particularly with the Revio 226 

platform, yields more comprehensive reconstructions of microbial genomes with enhanced continuity and 227 

more complete contigs. 228 

Ultra-deep sequencing with paired-end Illumina technology generated 903.3 million reads of 2x150 base pairs 229 

in length. Combining PacBio HiFi-LRsequencing using a full SMRTcell on Sequel II and another on Revio 230 

platforms yielded a lower total of 11.2 million reads, but with an average length of 7.25 kb, and a maximum 231

Reads were assembled using technology-specific pipelines to ultimately reconstruct the genomes of soil 235

persisted at the assembly level, with Revio assemblies containing three times more assembled base pairs in 244

Four contigs obtained from Sequel II reads were longer than 1 Mb, with the longest being 1.5Mb in length. 246 

In contrast, assembly of Revio reads yielded 139 contigs of at least 1 Mb, and 87 longer than the longest 247 

Sequel II contig. The longest contig obtained with Revio was 5.7 Mb long, demonstrating the capacity of this 248 

technology to deliver HiFi-LRs with higher coverage useful for resolving microbial genomes from complex 249 

environments. Although Illumina yielded nearly twice the total number of base pairs compared to PacBio at 250

620 bp and a maximum contig length of 185 kb (Table 1, Fig. 1). The Revio platform, by generating more 252 

HiFi-LRs, enables the assembly of contigs longer than those generated not only from Illumina data but also 253 

from Sequel II data. 254 

The improvement in contig lengths generated from long reads is expected to positively impact the downstream 255 

completeness of gene predictions. De novo gene prediction from long-read assemblies yielded approximately 18 256 

million proteins classified as complete, representing 80% of all predictions, compared to fewer than 5 million 257 

(12% of all predicted proteins) from SR contigs. Furthermore, the length distributions of predicted proteins 258 

are consistent with those reported by Never and colleagues [ 54 ] for high-quality predicted proteomes (Supp 259 

Fig. 1B). Indeed, in this work, the authors showed that protein length distribution is a reliable indicator of 260 

genome assembly quality, with conserved protein lengths typically mostly ranging from 236 to 353 amino 261 

acids (726 to 1059 base pairs) across different domains of the tree of life (archaea: 242 amino acids, bacteria: 262 

270 amino acids, eukaryotes: 353 amino acids). This increased protein prediction accuracy is observed in 263 

both the LR contigs and the predictions from raw LR reads. 264 

Although HiFi-LRs generated by the Revio platform yielded better assembly metrics and gene predictions 265 

than short reads, combining the two long-read datasets (Sequel II + Revio, sq+rv) further enhanced assembly 266 

metrics. This suggests a direct positive effect of HiFi-LR sequencing depth on the assembly statistics. While 267 

the contig length is only slightly improved (Fig. 1, Supp. Fig. 2), the number of contigs and total base pairs 268 

incorporated in contigs for the pooled sq+rv dataset have significantly increased (Table 1). These results 269 

confirm the importance of sequencing depth in reconstructing DNA sequences from complex environments 270 

[55]. 271

Unmapped reads indicate limitations in coverage and assembly, mainly in low-abundance 272 

species. 273 

Unmapped reads are those that fail to align with the assembly either because they contain sequencing errors 274 

or are not represented in any contig. Characterising unmapped reads is crucial for assessing sequencing com-275 

pleteness and identifying genomic regions (or even species) that are challenging to assemble. In metagenomics, 276 

those unmapped reads often originate from low-abundant populations, leading to undetected species or highly 277 

fragmented bins. 278 

Here, mapping statistics revealed significant differences between sequencing platforms. When Illumina 279 

SR exhibited an unmapped-read rate of 17.5% on their associated contigs, PacBio HiFi-LR demonstrated 280 

unmapped-read rates of 28%, 16% and 14% for contigs generated from Sequel II, Revio or both technologies 281 

pooled, respectively (Table 1, Fig. 1A). On the PacBio datasets, the higher sequencing depth yielded 282 

a significantly lower proportion of unmapped reads (p-value < 10 −5, Z >100 for all comparisons with a 283 

two-sample proportion Z-test), with a small to medium effect size, increasing with the difference in sequencing 284 

depth (cohen’s h: 0.06 between pooled and Revio, 0.29 between Revio and Sequel II, and 0.35 between pooled 285 

and Sequel II). Thus, the higher number of generated contigs and incorporated base pairs increased the 286 

diversity of genomic regions captured by the assembly. 287 

Further comparisons of reads (Supp. Fig. 3) highlighted that unassembled long reads (sq+rv) exhibited 288 

slightly shorter lengths than those participating in the assembly, with a median length of 6.3 and 7.0 kb, 289 

respectively; the distributions in the two groups differing significantly (Mann-Whitney, P < 2.2e -16 , two-tailed), 290 

but with a small effect (rank-biserial correlation, r=0.15). Unassembled reads also displayed a lower GC 291

## Using SR coverage improves binning of HiFi-LR contigs 307 

We then compared the number and completeness of genomes reconstructed after binning for both SR and 308 

HiFi-LR datasets, considering different mapping strategies prior to binning: (i) mapping Illumina SR to 309 

the short-read contigs (SR-SR), (ii) HiFi-LR to the HiFi-LR contigs (LR-LR), and (iii) Illumina SR to the 310

contig length distribution and lower % of unmapped reads, we consider only this dataset as representative of 313 

the HiFi-LR technology from now on. 314 

Comparative analysis of bins and MAGs from Illumina SR and PacBio HiFi-LR assemblies 315 

obtained through self-mapping coverage 316 

We first considered binning the HiFi-LR assembly using the mapping of the corresponding long reads to the 317 

contigs (Fig. 2A). Such binning led to reduced unbinned contig length (Fig. 2B) and generated more than 318 

twice the number of bins (2,587 vs 1,187) and nearly twice as many MAGs (252 vs 145) compared to binning 319 

the SR contigs with short-read mapping (Table 2, Fig. 2C). This improvement was also reflected by longer 320 

contigs (up to 6.7 Mbp vs. 192 Kbp), greater bin lengths after dereplication, and a substantially higher N50 321 

(487 Kbp vs. 9 Kbp), indicating improved contiguity (Table 2). Regarding MAGs, the HiFi-LR assembly 322 

produced substantially more high- and medium-quality MAGs (75 HQ and 169 MQ vs. 16 HQ and 128 MQ 323 

for SR), as well as a greater number of low-quality MAGs (176 vs. 51). Notably, HiFi-LR assemblies also 324 

generated significantly more near-complete MAGs (8 vs none), highlighting their potential for near-complete 325 

genome reconstruction, even in very complex microbiomes (Fig. 2C). 326 

However, these better assembly metrics came with an increase in contamination, as evidenced by a larger 327 

number of contaminated bins (>10% contamination estimates) in the HiFi-LR dataset (132 vs. 63 bins 328 

classified as contaminated). This pitfall is likely related to the insufficient coverage information in HiFi-LR 329 

bins, due to relatively limited sequencing depth (Table 1) and resulting in relatively homogeneous read 330 

abundance when mapped to contigs (Fig. 2A,C). Specifically, HiFi-LR self-mapping resulted in low coverage 331 

depths on contigs (mean: 3X; range: 0–310X, Fig 2A) and on MAGs (mean: 6X; range: 0–63X), limiting the 332 

resolution of the binning process. 333 

In contrast, the depth of SR sequencing led to broader coverage estimation. Self-mapping SRs on their 334 

corresponding contigs yielded substantially higher coverage, with mean depths of 33X on SR-assembled 335 

contigs (range: 5–17,156X, Fig. 2A) and 40X on MAGs (range: 10–254X). Notably, more than half of 336 

Illumina reads aligned stringently to HiFi-LR-assembled contigs, with coverage depths reaching up to 27,323X 337 

(for bins mean: 9X; for MAGs mean: 16X range: 1–126X). These results suggest that the limited depth of 338 

HiFi-LR self-mapping undermines binning accuracy and results in an increase in contaminated bins (Fig. 339 

2C). Consequently, we hypothesised that incorporating SR coverage into the HiFi-LR binning process could 340 

improve bin quality by enhancing binning resolution and reducing contamination. 341

# Discussion 490 

## HiFi-LR sequencing advances genome-resolved soil metagenomics 491 

Soil is among the most microbially diverse environments, with profound implications for global biogeochemical 492 

cycles and yet still under-characterised at the genomic level [ 1, 2]. Although amplicon sequencing and SR 493 

metagenomics have provided insight into microbial composition and function, genome-resolved understanding 494 

has lagged due to fragmentation, poor resolution of closely related strains, and challenges in assembling 495 

repetitive regions [ 11 , 15 ]. Recent advances in long-read technologies—particularly PacBio HiFi sequencing— 496 

have demonstrated their potential in the gut microbiome, enabling highly contiguous assemblies and more 497 

comprehensive recovery of the gene sets [ 18, 44 , 57 ]. Here, we explore their benefits in a soil microbiome 498 

sample, directly comparing the added value of several sequencing and binning strategies. We show that 499 

HiFi-LRs substantially improve assembly quality as well as MAG completeness and contiguity compared 500 

to Illumina SRs alone, even at moderate sequencing depth. This improvement is expected to support more 501 

precise taxonomic and functional annotation in future studies, providing a robust foundation for investigating 502 

microbe–microbe and microbe–environment interactions in soil. 503 

## Hybrid binning of HiFi-LR contigs with SR data enhances recovery and resolution 504 

## of MAGs 505 

Despite the benefits of long reads, MAG recovery from long-read assemblies can be constrained by low 506 

coverage and weak abundance signals, particularly for rare taxa. In our work, coverage estimates from 507 

HiFi-LR data alone were poorly effective for abundance-based binning, even though an entire Sequel II and 508 

entire Revio SMRT Cell were used for sequencing of a unique sample. This low coverage signal resulted in 509 

contamination and collapsed bins. To address these challenges, we used the high-coverage Illumina SR data 510 

to generate coverage profiles for HiFi-assembled contigs. This hybrid binning strategy significantly improved 511 

bin quality—reducing contamination, increasing dereplication, and resulting in a 24% increase in MAG yield. 512 

Notably, this approach enabled the recovery of additional MAGs from low-abundance and taxonomically 513 

underrepresented lineages, including 34 genera not detected with other strategies and several lacking close 514 

matches in reference databases. We also observed improved resolution in phyla such as Acidobacteriota and 515 

Planctomycetota, consistent with previous successes of hybrid methods in less complex microbiomes [ 16 ]. We 516 

note, however, that MAG counts alone are not a comprehensive quality measure and that bin segmentation, 517 

strain heterogeneity and contig taxonomic discordance within bins are additional valuable metrics to consider. 518

## Limitations and future perspectives 534 

Despite the improvements offered by HiFi-LR sequencing and hybrid binning, the number of recovered MAGs 535 

remains substantially lower than the taxonomic richness estimated by amplicon-based profiling. For complex 536 

communities such as soil, the absence of a ground truth makes comprehensive benchmarking challenging. 537 

Current standards rely on mock or synthetic communities [ 58], which do not fully capture environmental 538 

complexity. In this study, we use metabarcoding data as a proxy to estimate an upper bound for microbial 539 

diversity, while acknowledging the known limitations of this approach, including amplification bias, copy 540 

number variation, and taxonomic assignment inaccuracies [ 56 ]. The discrepancy between 16S rRNA gene-541

greater in soil due to its extreme heterogeneity and prevalence of low-abundance or recalcitrant taxa. It is 543

A technical constraint in soil metagenomics is the relatively short length of extracted DNA, rarely exceeding 554 

6 kb, which is well below the 30–40 kb typically obtained from gut or aquatic samples [ 59]. This limits the 555

DNA fragments from challenging samples, and continued expansion of environmental genome catalogs 561 

will be essential. Together, these improvements will enable more comprehensive and functionally resolved 562 

characterisation of soil microbial communities, contributing to our understanding of their roles in nutrient 563 

cycling, soil health, and ecosystem resilience. 564 

# Conclusion 565 

Collectively, our results demonstrate that integrating PacBio HiFi-LR sequencing with SR coverage for 566 

binning markedly enhances the recovery, resolution, and diversity of MAGs from complex soil environments. 567 

This hybrid strategy represents a step toward closing the gap between microbial diversity estimated with 568 

amplicon-based profiling and that captured by shotgun metagenomic assembly, facilitating access to previously 569 

undetected lineages and enabling a more comprehensive taxonomic characterization of soil microbiomes. By 570 

leveraging the high contiguity of long reads and the depth provided by short reads, the hybrid approach 571 

improves binning accuracy and supports the recovery of a broader range of genomes, including those from 572 

low-abundance taxa. 573 

Importantly, while HiFi-LR sequencing alone offers advantages in assembly quality, the addition of short-read 574 

data for binning substantially increases the resolution and completeness of recovered MAGs. Our findings 575 

underscore the potential of this hybrid method both for enhancing new studies and, whenever samples are 576 

still available, for adding value to existing SR datasets. However, the benefits of this approach are contingent 577 

on adequate sequencing depth, which remains a limiting factor, particularly for the recovery of rare taxa in 578 

highly complex soil microbiomes. 579 

In summary, the integration of HiFi-LR and SR sequencing represents a robust and scalable strategy for 580 

genome-resolved exploration of complex microbial ecosystems. As sequencing technologies and library 581 

preparation methods continue to advance, and as comprehensive reference catalogues expand, the potential for 582 

soil metagenomics to reveal the functional roles and interactions within microbial communities will continue 583 

to grow. 584
