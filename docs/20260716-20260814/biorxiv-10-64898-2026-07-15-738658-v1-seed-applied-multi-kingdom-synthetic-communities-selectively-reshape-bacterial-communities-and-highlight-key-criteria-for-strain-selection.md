## 摘要
定义微生物群落（也称为合成群落）在植物健康方面显示出有前景的结果，但缺乏从实验室到田间的有效转化。这是由于在考虑复杂环境和多界相互作用的情况下，如何有效调控植物微生物群的知识有限。在本研究中，我们旨在更好地理解多界合成群落（SynComs）从种子到幼苗阶段的传播和影响。我们使用先验和随机两种方法，从包括24种细菌、11种酵母和10种丝状真菌的多样菌株库中构建了20个不同的SynComs。将SynComs接种到甘蓝型油菜种子上，并监测其在非无菌土壤中生长的15天龄幼苗微生物群上的传播和影响。接种方案的优化表明，与其他方法相比，海藻酸盐包衣使细菌、酵母和丝状真菌的浓度提高了2个对数以上。使用这种接种方法，我们观察到对比鲜明的幼苗定殖谱，SynComs成员占细菌群落的1.1-45.7%，占真菌群落的3.2-36.6%。我们的多种SynCom设计揭示，菌株选择是SynCom性能比组装策略更关键的决定因素。即使是随机组装的群落也表现良好，只要它们来自生态相关、适应性良好的分类群库。基于这些证据，我们确定了关键的细菌和真菌性状，如接种种子上的高丰度和低体外迟滞期，这些性状解释了有效的幼苗定殖。尽管定殖水平较低，我们观察到SynCom接种改变了14个SynComs中幼苗细菌群落的组装。共有82个本地细菌ASVs被确定为对SynCom接种有响应，最可能源自土壤。这种转变表明，SynComs通过调节环境类群的招募来影响群落组装，特别是当SynCom菌株更整合在多界网络结构中时。最后，我们确定了四种不同的SynCom谱，它们要么强烈定殖幼苗，要么不定殖，同时改变或不改变本地微生物群。总之，这些发现为改进SynCom设计提供了可操作的指导，表明利用宿主适应、最佳接种密度和网络整合等生态过程可以增强定殖效率和植物表型结果。

## Abstract
Defined microbial communities (also known as synthetic communities) are showing promising results for plant health but are lacking an efficient lab to field transition. This is the due to limited knowledge on how to efficiently modulate plant microbiota by considering complex environments and multi-kingdom interactions. In this study, we aimed to better understand the transmission and impact of multi-kingdom synthetic communities (SynComs) from the seed to seedling stage. We constructed 20 different SynComs using both a priori and random approaches, from pool of diverse strains including 24 bacteria, 11 yeasts and 10 filamentous fungi. SynComs were inoculated on Brassica napus seeds and we monitored both transmission and impact on the microbiota of 15-day-old seedlings grown in non-sterile soil. Optimization of the inoculation protocol showed that alginate coating improved bacterial, yeast and filamentous fungi concentrations by more than 2 log compared with other approaches. With this inoculation method, we observed contrasted seedling colonization profiles, with SynComs members representing between 1.1-45.7% of bacterial community and 3.2-36.6% of fungal community. Our multiple SynCom design revealed that strain selection is a more critical determinant of SynCom performance than assembly strategy. Even randomly assembled communities performed well, as long as they are drawn from a pool of ecologically relevant, well-adapted taxa. Based on these evidences, we identified key bacterial and fungal traits explaining efficient seedling colonization such as high abundance on inoculated seed and low in vitro lag-time. Despite low colonization levels, we observed that SynCom inoculation altered seeding bacterial community assembly in 14 SynComs. A total of 82 native bacterial ASVs were identified as responsive to SynCom inoculation, most likely originating from the soil. This shift indicates that SynComs influence community assembly by modulating the recruitment of environmental taxa, especially when SynCom strains were more integrated in multi-kingdom network structures. Finally, we identified four distinct SynComs profiles which either colonized strongly or not seedlings while shifting of not native microbiota. Altogether, these findings provide actionable directions for improving SynCom design, suggesting that leveraging ecological processes such as host adaptation, optimal inoculation density, and network integration could enhance both colonization efficiency and plant phenotypic outcomes.
