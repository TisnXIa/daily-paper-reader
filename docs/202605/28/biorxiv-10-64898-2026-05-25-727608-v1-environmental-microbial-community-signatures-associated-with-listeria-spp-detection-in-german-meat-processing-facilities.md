## 摘要
单核细胞增生李斯特菌（Listeria monocytogenes）即使在常规清洁消毒后仍可在肉类加工环境的生态位中持续存在。其持久性不仅取决于胁迫耐受性，还可能依赖于与常驻微生物群落的相互作用——这些群落可能促进或抑制其存活。然而，人们对这些生态关系仍知之甚少。我们结合16S rRNA V3/V4扩增子测序、基于培养的检测和多位点序列分型（MLST），在一年内对六家德国肉类加工设施中的微生物群落进行了表征。我们考察了群落结构、采样点（排水沟和食品接触表面）与李斯特菌属（包括单核细胞增生李斯特菌）检出之间的关联。微生物群落以食品加工环境中典型的核心属为主，尤其是假单胞菌属（Pseudomonas）和不动杆菌属（Acinetobacter），但不同设施间存在显著差异（PERMANOVA, p = 0.001；配对R² = 0.023-0.079），表明存在设施特异性群落。基于培养的分析在370份环境样本中的51份（13.8%）中检出李斯特菌，主要来自排水沟（44/51，86.3%）。六个设施中有五个检出单核细胞增生李斯特菌，21株分离株中的19株来自排水沟（90.5%）。对74株可进行MLST分型的单核细胞增生李斯特菌分离株进行MLST分析，发现高度多样性，涵盖15个克隆复合体中的21种序列型，其中谱系II占主导地位（86.5%）。总体微生物群落组成与李斯特菌属和单核细胞增生李斯特菌的存在显著相关（PERMANOVA, p = 0.001；R² = 0.0137和0.0083）。在排水沟中，归属于不动杆菌属、Rhizorhapis属和漫游球菌属（Vagococcus）的ASV与李斯特菌属阳性样本呈正相关。这些发现共同表明，排水沟是李斯特菌属的关键生态位，而相关分类群可能指示与李斯特菌属检出相关的排水沟群落。

## Abstract
Listeria monocytogenes can persist in niches of meat processing environments despite routine cleaning and disinfection. Its persistence may depend not only on stress tolerance but also on interactions with resident microbial communities, which may promote or inhibit survival. However, these ecological relationships remain poorly understood. We combined 16S rRNA V3/V4 amplicon sequencing, culture-based detection, and multilocus sequence typing (MLST) to characterize microbial communities in six German meat processing facilities over one year. We examined associations among community structure, sampling sites (drains and food-contact surfaces), and the occurrence of Listeria spp., including L. monocytogenes. Microbial communities were dominated by core genera typical of food-processing environments, particularly Pseudomonas spp. and Acinetobacter spp., but differed significantly among facilities (PERMANOVA, p = 0.001; pairwise R{superscript 2} = 0.023-0.079), indicating facility-specific communities. Culture-based analyses detected Listeria spp. in 51 of 370 environmental samples (13.8%), mainly from drains (44/51, 86.3%). L. monocytogenes was detected in five of six facilities, with 19 of 21 isolates originating from drains (90.5%). MLST of 74 typeable L. monocytogenes isolates revealed high diversity, comprising 21 sequence types across 15 clonal complexes, with lineage II predominating (86.5%). Overall microbial community composition was significantly associated with Listeria spp. and L. monocytogenes presence (PERMANOVA, p = 0.001; R{superscript 2} = 0.0137 and 0.0083). In drains, ASVs assigned to Acinetobacter, Rhizorhapis, and Vagococcus species showed positive associations with Listeria spp.-positive samples. Together these findings suggest that drains are key ecological niches for Listeria spp. and that associated taxa may indicate drain communities linked to Listeria spp. recovery.
